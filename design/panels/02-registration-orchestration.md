# Panel 2: Registration orchestration and honest-split lane routing

## The fork

FR-5.0's request-level attempt loop sits above FR-8.1's (user,event) granularity, and the FR-8.1 workflowId cannot exist until discovery produces canonical_event_id — what durable structure owns the request-level orchestration, the per-event registration saga, lane routing, policy/kill-switch enforcement, and fair-share pacing? Candidate shapes included a two-tier Temporal parent/child topology, a thin-workflow fat-services design with a stateless coordinator over Postgres, an event-driven outbox+broker choreography, and one flat workflow per EventRequest carrying registration + lifecycle inline.

## Decision

TWO-TIER TEMPORAL SPINE WITH SERVICE-PLANE PACING: a short-lived parent EventRequestWorkflow (workflowId=req:{tenant}:{request_id}) owns parse→discover→rank→conflict-gate and the FR-5.0 ≤3-attempt loop; per-(user_id,canonical_event_id) child RegistrationWorkflows (workflowId={user}:{event}, REJECT_DUPLICATE, ParentClosePolicy=ABANDON) own the FR-8.1 saga, within-candidate lane ladder, handoff-mode TTL wait, and months-long post-booking lifecycle. Policy/kill-switch is a Postgres-backed data-plane PDP evaluated as a policy_gate activity AND re-read inside every mutating activity immediately pre-wire-call (the guard, not signals, carries AC-50), with freeze-then-handoff grace semantics. Fair-share pacing (FR-10.4 + G2 contingency) is an off-engine Redis Pacer service with per-(source,credential) token buckets and a config-flip deficit-round-robin per-app degrade mode — Proposal 1's workflow topology with Proposal 2/3's pacing and policy substrates grafted in.

## Rationale

The fork's central tension — FR-5.0's request-level attempt loop sits above FR-8.1's (user,event) granularity, and the FR-8.1 workflowId cannot exist until discovery produces canonical_event_id — must be owned by SOMETHING durable. The parent workflow is the lowest-risk owner: (1) it satisfies FR-6.8/AC-48's letter ("intake starts exactly one durable workflow") which P2 concedes trading and P3 fails identically; (2) the attempt counter and start-child-then-record window are exactly the crash-critical state P2 must hand-protect with SELECT FOR UPDATE + outbox redelivery + bespoke crash-injection CI — engine journaling eliminates that hand-rolled class, and the requirements already settled Temporal as the spine to avoid it; (3) P2's quantified savings are immaterial: ~2-3× lower Temporal action volume ≈ low-$1k/mo against the NFR-5 envelope (~520k RSVPs/mo × ≤$0.20 blended ≈ $104k/mo), and the versioning-hostile surface it protects is the PARENT, which closes within hours so patched code drains in days — the months-lived versioned surface (child lifecycle) is identical in both designs; (4) engine load verifies with headroom: worst-case ~1,600 transitions/s at the 750 req/min Friday peak (d15 line 49) vs Temporal's tens-of-thousands/s (d13 finding 6), and ~240k open children hold zero worker resources during durable waits (d13 finding 3).

Two of P1's edges are rejected on their own weakness: Temporal-native QuotaWorkflows put an engine Update round-trip (~50-200ms + billed action) in front of every source call and sprawl to ~100k entity workflows, when pacing is politeness-not-correctness (fail-safe direction = throttle-first) and the browser-pool admission cap needs a service anyway — Redis DRR (P2/P3) is sub-ms and carries the G2 per-app flip as pure config; and OPA-sidecar batch-signal fan-out is demoted because the pre-mutate guard alone is the AC-50 mechanism (a signal is only observed at a workflow-task boundary and cannot stop a dispatched activity — verified true in all three proposals' analysis). P3 is rejected as base: broker+relay+inbox is net-new ops surface for a v1, its own risks concede the NFR-1 p95≤5s read path may abandon the spine, and closing the registration workflow at `scheduled` to spawn per-change reconcile workflows contradicts §3 (scheduled is not terminal) and fragments FR-8.1.

The FR-8.2 literal placement (discover/rank as steps of the per-event workflow) is structurally impossible for ANY proposal at FR-8.1's own granularity; the decided shape keeps those steps as journaled activities in the request-scoped workflow directly above — the closest achievable reading, recorded as an ADR interpretation, not an exception.

## Decided mechanism

- Intake Gateway (FastAPI + Temporal client): parses email/SMS/API intake (FR-6.8), dedups on hash(tenant, normalized_text, time_bucket) which deterministically mints request_id; writes a tiny Postgres start-outbox row then StartWorkflow(EventRequestWorkflow, reject-duplicate) so a Temporal Cloud blip never loses an inbound request (replay is dedup-safe). Un-RSVP (FR-6.7) and handoff mark-done are SignalWorkflow to {user}:{event}.
- EventRequestWorkflow (parent, workflowId=req:{tenant}:{request_id}, history ~40-60 events, closes within hours): journaled activities parse_request (Haiku) → discover fan-out per SourcePort (catalog cache for TM/SerpApi per d15; per-user-token Meetup/Luma reads via Pacer, FR-3.5) → dedupe_canonical → rank (d07 stack) → hard-constraint filter + freeBusy hard gate (FR-4.5, hard-overlap ⇒ BLOCKED, never attempted, AC-27). RankedCandidateSet pinned to S3 (claim-check), key in workflow state. Attempt loop: counter ≤3 in workflow state; per attempt, start child with the candidate's lane_plan, await its outcome signal, fall through on failure. Terminal per FR-5.0: direct the best handoff-eligible candidate's child into handoff-mode, else failed_no_candidate + FR-6.6 notification (AC-33).
- RegistrationWorkflow (child, workflowId={user_id}:{canonical_event_id}, WorkflowIdReusePolicy=REJECT_DUPLICATE, ParentClosePolicy=ABANDON, search attributes: canonical_event_id, tenant, lane, lifecycle_state): load_selection (re-fetch the single candidate for freshness + freeBusy re-check) → resolve_membership (FR-8.6) → policy_gate activity → lane ladder per lane_plan → await_confirmation (durable timer 24h + signal, FR-8.4) → dedupe_calendar → write_to_calendar → durable lifecycle wait until event_end+grace handling organizer_change/un_rsvp/policy_freeze/handoff_completed signals → exactly one terminal state per §3. Handoff-mode: create_handoff_task activity (task row + deep link + outbox in one Postgres tx, <1s from routing decision vs NFR-2 60s) → 7-day TTL timer + completion signal → FR-6.3 freeBusy re-check → calendar write, or expired terminal.
- Lane ladder = within-candidate attempt semantics (AC-32): the child receives an ordered lane_plan for its CanonicalEvent (autonomous-on-SLA → browser-best-effort → handoff-eligible flag) and exhausts autonomous+browser lanes before reporting ONE failed attempt to the parent — attempts are per-candidate, lane fall-through is within-candidate.
- Directive-signal protocol (resolves REJECT_DUPLICATE vs FR-5.0 terminal handoff): on lane exhaustion the child signals the parent outcome=failed(reason, handoff_eligible) and parks on a directive wait (timer 10 min); parent replies close (fall-through continues) or demote-to-handoff (this child becomes the request's terminal handoff — no second workflow needed for an already-tried event); timer expiry defaults to close.
- LaneRouter: ONE pure, versioned, workflow-safe decision function route(source, automation_allowed{api,browser}, group_condition, spike_flags) evaluated twice — advisory in the parent for candidate/lane ordering, authoritative in the child against a fresh policy-snapshot activity result after resolve_membership. Table: Meetup∧api∧member∧¬(approval|screening|dues)→AUTONOMOUS_SLA; Meetup∧api∧open-instant-join→AUTONOMOUS_SLA only if meetup_autojoin spike flag (default FALSE, d17); Meetup∧(non-member|approval|screening|dues)→HANDOFF w/ join deep link; Meetup∧browser→REFUSED (FR-3.2); Luma∧browser∧credential-healthy→BROWSER_BEST_EFFORT else HANDOFF; Luma∧api→REFUSED (no user-side API, d10); Eventbrite→HANDOFF always (ToS §13.1, SerpApi discovery); Ticketmaster→HANDOFF deep link; Partiful→DISABLED/skip. Inputs are DATA (policy snapshot); function changes go through Temporal patch-versioning; unit-tested against the AC-34 fixture.
- PolicyGate (FR-5.9/7.2, three data-plane layers): (1) parent pre-skip during selection; (2) policy_gate activity before register reading the Postgres policy store (automation_allowed per source×modality, per-user RSVPs/period counters, paid_allowed, kill_switch global+per-user) via an in-process deterministic PDP (OPA/Cedar-compatible rule shape per d04) with LISTEN/NOTIFY cache push, ≤2s propagation; (3) THE AC-50 MECHANISM: a deterministic non-LLM pre-mutate guard as the first instruction of every mutating activity re-reads the policy cache immediately before the wire call and raises non-retryable policy_denied. Evaluation error or store outage = DENY, fail-closed to handoff (NFR-15). Kill-switch engage = one row flip, no deploy, no workflow enumeration.
- Kill-switch freeze semantics (grafted from P3): on policy_denied(killswitch) the workflow PARKS, re-checking on a 60s durable timer for a bounded grace window (default 30 min); disengage within grace resumes normal evaluation (AC-50 disengage semantics); grace expiry short-circuits to handoff (FR-5.9, NFR-15). Zero mutations occur post-engage regardless (the guard fires pre-wire-call).
- Pacer (off-engine fair-share scheduler, FR-10.4): Redis token buckets keyed (source, credential) — meetup:{user_token} 500pts/60s, luma:{calendar} 100 POST/5min with 429⇒1-min full-block honor, tm:{app_key} 5rps+5,000/day for the crawler — deficit-round-robin fair-share across users within a bucket, honoring retry-after/resetAt. Mutating and per-user-discovery activities acquire a lease pre-call; long projected waits convert to durable-timer backoff in the workflow rather than holding a worker slot. Browser-pool admission cap (~135) lives here too. Redis loss re-initializes buckets EMPTY (throttle-first, fail-safe direction).
- G2 per-app contingency (config flip, no re-architecture): quota_scope[meetup]=app collapses to ONE global 500pts/60s bucket, DRR over per-user FIFO lanes (~33 RSVP chains/min at the ~15pts/chain d15 estimate — TO BE MEASURED in the spike); projected wait > 5 min ⇒ DEGRADE ⇒ child routes that lane to handoff and the autonomous-Meetup SLA declaration flips to none — exactly the FR-10.4/G2 posture (global fair-share + handoff, no fixed SLA).
- Source adapters as activities: MeetupAdapter — read_membership, read_rsvp_state (FR-5.3, CONFIRMED⇒no-op), create_rsvp (createEventRsvp, member_id OMITTED, user's own OAuth token per d17), delete_rsvp; distinct typed errors (RATE_LIMITED / consumer-revoked / auth / ban) per d17. LumaBrowserAdapter — browser_detect and browser_submit as SEPARATE activities inside the settled d14 microVM+injection-broker envelope. create_rsvp and browser_submit carry RetryPolicy maximumAttempts=1: ALL retry paths are explicit workflow loops re-entering through read_rsvp_state/browser_detect — blind re-POST is structurally unrepresentable (FR-5.3/5.5).
- Idempotency keys minted ONCE into a workflow-state key dict at child start and passed into activities, never generated inside a retried activity (FR-8.3, d13 finding 4): source-mutation key f"{workflow_id}:{run_id}:{step}:{seq}"; calendar event id = base32hex(hash(tenant_id + canonical_event_id)) computed in workflow code, identical in every code path (FR-9.2 — cross-source re-registration collides at the primary key); compensations reuse the same minted keys.
- Compensation layout: recorded compensation stack in child workflow state (delete_rsvp / delete_calendar_entry / withdrawal-handoff), each idempotent under its minted key. EXCEPTION (grafted from P2): calendar-write permanent failure AFTER a confirmed RSVP is FORWARD recovery — manual-confirm handoff task + notification, never auto-un-RSVP (the registration is the valuable effect; the deterministic calendar key makes the eventual write converge). Free-only launch means no non-reversible pivot exists in v1 (d04 pivot logic reserved for the stubbed payment port).
- Claim-check Payload Codec on every worker auto-offloads payloads >256 KB to S3 with per-tenant prefixes (FR-8.5) — the 100-200-candidate RRF set (~400-800 KB), screenshots, raw DOM always offload; hard margin under the 2MB payload/4MB gRPC limits (d13 finding 2).
- Lifecycle projection + transactional outbox (FR-8.9): record_transition activity commits lifecycle row + outbox row in ONE Postgres transaction at every state change; relay publishes to idempotent consumers deduped on (workflow_id, transition_seq) — audit log (FR-7.3), notifications (FR-6.6 at-least-once + dedup), dashboards. Postgres is a rebuildable read projection (NFR-13); Temporal history is execution truth. The outbox is the mandated delivery mechanism, NOT the inter-stage transport.
- Lifecycle Watchers: Temporal Schedules run per-source pollers (Meetup poll, Luma poll + RelayInbox change-email, TM catalog-crawl delta, handoff-event poll) on the ≤6h NFR-17 cadence; on eventStatus change, visibility query (canonical_event_id=X AND lifecycle_state IN registered,scheduled) → coalesced organizer_change signal to each open child, which runs the reconcile branch (update/remove calendar entry via minted key, notify, terminal cancelled/rescheduled). Children continue-as-new after reconcile bursts to bound history.
- RelayInbox Signaler (settled d16): SES→S3→Lambda, sender-domain allowlist, deterministic OTP/magic-link extraction (no raw HTML to any LLM, FR-5.7/10.6), SignalWorkflow({user}:{event}) for confirmations, handoff-completion emails, and organizer change-emails.
- Launch parameters: attempt budget 3 (FR-5.0, ratified); confirmation timeout 24h; handoff TTL 7d (ratified); child directive-wait 10 min; kill-switch grace 30 min; G2 DEGRADE threshold 5 min; browser submit loop counter ≤2; browser task-queue ScheduleToStartTimeout 120s (saturation⇒handoff, AC-45); organizer-change poll ≤6h; claim-check 256KB; policy propagation ≤2s. Load basis: 750 req/min peak, ~390 child starts/min (0.4 conv × 1.3 attempts), worst-case ~1,600 engine transitions/s, ~240k open lifecycle children, browser pool ~135.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Thin-workflow fat-services (P2 as base): stateless RequestCoordinator + Postgres request-state row + outbox owning the FR-5.0 loop, Temporal scoped to one RegisterSaga** — Trades the FR-6.8/AC-48 letter (intake "starts the durable workflow") and its own remediation — a thin per-request Temporal wrapper — concedes the parent workflow while keeping a now-redundant hand-rolled state machine. The attempt counter and start-child-then-commit window are crash-critical state requiring bespoke SELECT FOR UPDATE + redelivery + crash-injection rigor that engine journaling gives for free; the quantified savings don't justify it (Temporal action delta ≈ low-$1k/mo vs the ~$104k/mo NFR-5 envelope at 100k users; the versioning surface it protects is the hours-lived parent, while the months-lived child versioning burden is identical in both designs). Verified honest otherwise — its Pacer, policy mechanism, and compensation stance are grafted in.
- **Event-driven choreography (P3): outbox+broker spine between stages, Selection Coordinator reducer, Temporal scoped to per-event register sagas** — Three verified defects: (1) internal inconsistency on FR-8.1/§3 — the flow closes the registration workflow at `scheduled` (NOT a terminal state per §3) and spawns per-change user:event:reconcile:{hash} workflows, fragmenting the one-workflow-per-(user,event) lifecycle its own component description promises to hold open; (2) its own risks concede NFR-1 (p95 ≤5s ranked list) may force the intake→discovery→ranking path OFF the broker spine into request/response with journal-only events — paying for a broker that isn't the transport; (3) NATS/Kafka + relay + inbox tables are net-new ops surface (DLQ handling, redelivery tuning, schema governance across ~10 consumer groups) on top of settled Temporal Cloud for a v1 launch, when FR-8.9's outbox already yields the audit-for-free property as a projection without being the drive shaft.
- **Temporal-native QuotaWorkflows (P1's pacing edge): per-(source,credential) entity RateLimiterWorkflows granting leases via Update** — Puts an engine round-trip (~50-200ms + a billed action) in front of every source call (~3 acquires per Meetup RSVP chain), sprawls to ~100k long-lived entity workflows at scale, and buys durability that pacing doesn't need — rate-limiting is politeness, not correctness; the fail-safe direction on state loss is throttle-first re-initialization, which a Redis bucket does trivially. The browser-pool admission cap and Luma 429-block bookkeeping need a fast service-plane component anyway; d15 directive 3 mandates the scheduler, not the substrate.
- **One flat workflow per EventRequest carrying registration + lifecycle inline (the fork's third named option)** — Violates FR-8.1's granularity outright — no {user}:{event} workflowId or reject-duplicate dedup across concurrent requests targeting the same event (AC-55 unmeetable), and a single history spanning the ≤3-attempt loop plus a months-long post-booking lifecycle has no clean continue-as-new seam. No proposal argued it; rejected on the binding constraint, not convenience.
- **Mandatory OPA-sidecar fleet + batch-signal fan-out as the kill-switch mechanism (P1's policy edge)** — The batch signal to ~240k open workflows is multi-second-to-minutes fan-out and a signal is only observed at a workflow-task boundary — it can never stop a dispatched activity, so it cannot carry AC-50 (P1 itself concedes the guard is the correctness mechanism). With the pre-mutate guard mandatory, the sidecar fleet + signal machinery adds deployment surface without adding a guarantee; Postgres-backed policy data + LISTEN/NOTIFY + in-process deterministic PDP (d04-compatible) reaches the same ≤2s guard effectiveness with the per-user counters already living in Postgres. An optional drain signal can be added later as operational UX.

## Salvaged ideas

- From P2/P3: the Pacer as an off-engine Redis token-bucket + deficit-round-robin service keyed (source,credential), with the G2 per-app contingency as a pure config flip (quota_scope[meetup]=app) and DEGRADE-to-handoff on projected-wait threshold.
- From P2: forward-recovery (manual-confirm handoff, never auto-un-RSVP) as the compensation for calendar-write failure after a confirmed registration — the registration is the valuable effect.
- From P2: pinning the RankedCandidateSet as an immutable claim-check snapshot plus a load_selection freshness re-check (re-fetch the single candidate + freeBusy re-check) in the child before register, bounding staleness across the ≤3-attempt loop.
- From P2/P3: the in-activity pre-mutate policy re-check as the FIRST instruction of every mutating activity, backed by Postgres + LISTEN/NOTIFY — adopted as THE AC-50 mechanism (P1 had the same guard but hedged it behind OPA sidecars + batch signals).
- From P3: freeze-then-handoff kill-switch semantics — park on a 60s re-check timer for a bounded grace window (30 min) before short-circuiting to handoff, reconciling FR-7.2's disengage-resumes with FR-5.9's short-circuit and NFR-15.
- From P3: the explicit REFUSED rows in the lane-router decision table (meetup∧browser, luma∧api) making ToS-prohibited modalities structurally unreachable, and the lane_plan formulation of within-candidate lane ordering.
- From P1: the intake Postgres start-outbox so a Temporal Cloud blip never loses an inbound request (replayed StartWorkflow calls are reject-duplicate-safe), and the search-attribute scheme (canonical_event_id, tenant, lane, lifecycle_state) that makes reconcile fan-out a visibility query.

## Edge cases

- Child start hits WorkflowExecutionAlreadyStarted (concurrent request targets the same (user,event)): parent queries the existing execution's lifecycle_state — registered/scheduled ⇒ request satisfied by dedup (terminal satisfied); in-flight ⇒ skip to next-ranked candidate; closed-as-failed ⇒ skip candidate within this request (REJECT_DUPLICATE per FR-8.1).
- User explicitly re-requests an event whose (user,event) workflow previously closed failed: REJECT_DUPLICATE blocks a restart, so the safe default is a handoff task with deep link (NFR-15-compliant); the ALLOW_DUPLICATE_FAILED_ONLY relaxation is listed for owner ratification.
- Terminal-handoff candidate was already tried in the attempt loop (its child is open, parked on the directive wait): parent sends demote-to-handoff to the EXISTING child instead of starting a second workflow — the directive-signal protocol (outcome signal → 10-min directive wait → close|demote, timer defaults to close) makes this race-free under FR-8.1.
- Kill-switch engages with a register activity already dispatched to a worker: the pre-mutate guard re-reads policy ≤2s-fresh immediately before the wire call and raises non-retryable policy_denied — zero post-engage mutations (AC-50); the workflow then parks (60s re-check, 30-min grace) and short-circuits to handoff on grace expiry; disengage within grace resumes (AC-40).
- Browser submit ACK lost (worker dies / timeout after click): submit has maximumAttempts=1 so the ENGINE never retries it; the workflow loop re-runs browser_detect in a fresh microVM — CONFIRMED ⇒ idempotent no-op, proceed; NOT_PRESENT ⇒ at most one more submit (loop counter ≤2); AMBIGUOUS ⇒ create_handoff_task(reason=ambiguous_state) + human-review signal wait — blind re-POST is structurally unreachable (FR-5.5/AC-37).
- Meetup read_rsvp_state returns CONFIRMED (user RSVPed manually out-of-band): idempotent no-op success (FR-5.3/AC-36); the saga proceeds directly to dedupe_calendar → write_to_calendar so the calendar leg still completes.
- Candidate goes stale between rank and attempt k (sold out, rescheduled, new calendar conflict): child's load_selection re-fetches the single candidate and re-runs freeBusy + hard constraints before any mutation; violation ⇒ outcome=failed(stale) with NO source mutation ⇒ parent falls through (FR-4.5's gate is enforced at execution time, not just selection time).
- Unexpected paywall with paid_allowed=false (mis-tagged free event, donation wall): the activity aborts pre-submit with typed free_paid_discrepancy, records the discrepancy via outbox, routes to handoff (FR-5.10/AC-41).
- Meetup consumer revocation / ban / RATE_LIMITED mid-flight: adapter's typed error taxonomy (d17) drives a quarantine activity that flips the source's automation_allowed row (FR-10.3); in-flight children fail their next policy check ⇒ handoff; LaneRouter stops routing new work within one config reload — no autonomous-lane SLA breach (AC-44), no retry against the banned surface, no IP rotation path exists (AC-72).
- Browser pool saturation at the Friday peak (>~135 concurrent): Pacer admission cap + ScheduleToStartTimeout=120s on the browser task queue ⇒ the child routes to handoff rather than queueing past the SLA (AC-45); saturation is backpressure the workflow observes.
- Confirmation email never arrives: 24h durable timer wins the wait_condition race ⇒ create_handoff_task, zero worker resources held (FR-8.4/AC-57). Handoff ignored: 7-day TTL timer ⇒ terminal expired + outbox notification + workflow closed (FR-6.6/AC-46).
- Handoff completed but the user gained a conflicting commitment since routing: completion signal triggers the FR-6.3 freeBusy re-check ⇒ conflict-warning notification surfaced, lifecycle still advances per the requirement (AC-43).
- Organizer cancels while the child is in await_confirmation or handoff wait (not yet scheduled): organizer_change signal is handled at every await point ⇒ reconcile branch cancels the pending wait/handoff task, deletes any written calendar entry via the minted key, notifies, terminal cancelled (FR-8.7).
- Calendar write permanently fails after a confirmed RSVP: forward recovery — manual-confirm handoff + notification, registration retained (never auto-un-RSVP); the deterministic base32hex(tenant+canonical_event_id) id makes any later manual/retried write converge to one entry (FR-9.2/AC-65).
- Same CanonicalEvent registered via two different sources (cross-request): both paths compute the identical calendar id, colliding at the primary key into insert→409→patch — exactly one entry (AC-65).
- Duplicate intake (email provider re-delivery, user double-send): gateway dedup hash deterministically mints the same request_id ⇒ StartWorkflow reject-duplicate absorbs the replay ⇒ exactly one parent (AC-48).
- Worker crash at ANY saga step (AC-56 crash injection): Temporal replays from history — completed activities return recorded results; the ack-lost window is closed per-lane by read_rsvp_state / browser_detect re-entry plus the idempotent calendar upsert ⇒ exactly-once observable effects (NFR-8).
- Temporal Cloud outage: intake start-outbox buffers StartWorkflow/Signal calls for replay; in-flight state resumes losslessly on recovery (NFR-13); new handoff-task creation stalls for the window — the declared, owner-ratified NFR-2 exception.

## Open risks

- Temporal Cloud is a correlated failure domain for BOTH lanes: during an engine outage no NEW handoff task can be created (in-flight state resumes losslessly; intake buffers in the start-outbox), so NFR-2/NFR-15 time-to-handoff breaches for the outage window. Accepted deliberately over building a second non-Temporal handoff path; Temporal Cloud SLA vs NFR-3 99.9% must be verified at contract (O-6) — the "Cloud availability exceeds our target" claim is NOT dossier-backed.
- Temporal Cloud namespace throughput and action pricing are not dossier-backed: the ~$25/M-actions figure (→ ~$1.6-2.5k/mo estimate) and the worst-case ~1,600 transitions/s peak vs namespace APS limits both ride on public list figures; O-6 sizing/limits confirmation is the gate, with DBOS as the documented fallback (this shape ports: parent+child become two DBOS workflow functions).
- Workflow patch-versioning discipline: LaneRouter and the child's saga/lifecycle logic are replayed code across ~240k open executions; a sloppy patch is a non-determinism incident. Mitigated by pushing all volatility into policy DATA and keeping the decision function stable, but this is a standing operational competency requirement.
- Meetup point-cost (~15 pts/chain) inherits d15's flagged unpublished estimate; if real consumedPoints are 3-5× higher, per-token headroom shrinks and the G2 per-app global ceiling (~33 chains/min, already ~6× oversubscribed at 100k on P2's demand arithmetic) drops proportionally. The G2 spike MUST measure it before any autonomous-lane SLA number is set (already deferred per NFR-2).
- G1 request-mix is unmeasured: browser-pool sizing (~135), handoff-lane staffing, and the Meetup demand share (~50-60% assumed) are provisional until the G1 estimation gate runs.
- Child history bloat over months-long lifecycles from chatty pollers: mitigated by signal coalescing per event + continue-as-new after reconcile bursts, but this is an operational lint that must be load-tested, not assumed.
- Redis Pacer state is deliberately non-durable: the throttle-first re-initialization direction and resetAt honoring must be fault-injection-tested so a Redis loss can never produce an unthrottled burst against source quotas.
- The Meetup Pro OAuth consumer remains a sole-discretion revocable single point of failure for the only on-SLA register path (d17); the quarantine→handoff degradation is designed (AC-44) but the commercial-use-clause legal review (O-10) is still open.

## Owner ratification items

- Temporal-outage posture: accept that during a Temporal Cloud outage no new handoff task can be created (bounded NFR-2/NFR-15 breach for the outage window) rather than build a second non-Temporal handoff path; contingent on O-6 contract review confirming Temporal Cloud SLA ≥ NFR-3 99.9% and namespace throughput ≥ ~1,600 transitions/s peak — neither figure is dossier-backed.
- Temporal Cloud cost basis: the ~$25/M-actions list price (→ ~$1.6-2.5k/mo at 100k users) is an external figure, not dossier evidence — verify at contract under O-6 before treating the parent-workflow action volume as immaterial.
- Confirmation-wait timeout = 24h before handoff (FR-8.4 fixes the mechanism, not the number; 24h is the d13 design-implication example).
- Kill-switch grace window = 30 min freeze-before-handoff (reconciles FR-7.2 disengage-resumes with FR-5.9 short-circuit; a shorter window drains to handoff faster, a longer one preserves more in-flight work across brief engagements).
- G2 per-app DEGRADE threshold = 5 min projected queue wait before routing to handoff (sets the fair-share-lottery vs handoff-volume trade in the degraded mode).
- Re-request of a previously failed (user,event): default = handoff task (strict FR-8.1 REJECT_DUPLICATE); alternative = ALLOW_DUPLICATE_FAILED_ONLY reuse policy permitting one autonomous re-attempt — a deviation from FR-8.1's letter that only the owner can ratify.
- Minor loop parameters: child directive-wait 10 min (default close), browser submit loop counter ≤2, browser task-queue ScheduleToStartTimeout 120s.
- ADR interpretation to record: FR-8.2's discover→rank→select steps execute as journaled activities in the request-scoped PARENT workflow (structurally forced — the FR-8.1 workflowId requires canonical_event_id, discovery's output); and FR-6.8/AC-48's "starts the durable workflow" is satisfied by the parent EventRequestWorkflow at intake, with the FR-8.1 child starting at selection.
- Reaffirm the G2 spike as design-blocking for any SLA number: it must measure real consumedPoints per RSVP chain (the ~15pts figure is d15's unpublished estimate) and the quota scoping (per-token vs per-app) before the autonomous-lane SLA is declared (NFR-2 deferral).

## Proposals (for the record)

### Proposal 1

**Bias:** Temporal maximalist — one execution model. Every durable fact about an EventRequest lives in Temporal Cloud: a parent EventRequestWorkflow owns parse/discover/rank/select and the ≤3-candidate attempt loop; per-(user,event) child RegistrationWorkflows own the FR-8.1 saga plus post-booking lifecycle; lane routing is a versioned pure function executed as workflow code; policy/kill-switch reach workflows as signals PLUS a per-activity pre-mutate guard; fair-share pacing is Temporal-native (entity rate-limiter workflows + task-queue rate limits). No bespoke state machines, no external orchestrator DB — the workflow history IS the audit-grade record, replay-debuggable end to end.

**Summary:** One durable spine, two workflow tiers. A short-lived parent EventRequestWorkflow (workflowId=req:{tenant}:{request_id}) runs parse→discover→rank→conflict-gate as journaled activities, then executes the FR-5.0 attempt loop by starting per-event child RegistrationWorkflows (workflowId={user_id}:{canonical_event_id}, reject-duplicate, ParentClosePolicy=ABANDON) and awaiting a registration-phase-outcome signal from each; the child then lives on through await_confirmation, calendar write, and the months-long post-booking lifecycle (reconcile/un-RSVP) as durable waits. The lane router is a single versioned pure function over (source, modality-capability, group-condition, spike-flags) evaluated twice — advisorily in the parent at select time, authoritatively in the child against a fresh policy-snapshot activity immediately before register. Kill-switch and policy are belt-and-suspenders: an OPA-backed policy_check activity before every register, a batch signal to in-flight workflows for fast drain, and a deterministic non-LLM pre-mutate guard inside every mutating activity that re-reads the policy store — so engage produces zero further mutations without a deploy even for already-dispatched activities. Meetup read-before-mutate and browser detect-then-submit are separate activities with submit pinned to maximumAttempts=1, forcing every retry back through the read step at the workflow level, with idempotency keys minted once in workflow state. Fair-share pacing is Temporal-native: per-(source,credential) entity RateLimiterWorkflows granting token-bucket leases via Update, degrading to ONE global Meetup fair-share entity workflow (deficit-round-robin over tenants, wait-threshold→handoff) if the G2 spike proves the 500pt/60s quota is per-app.

### Proposal 2

**Bias:** thin-workflow fat-services — Temporal only where durability pays (the per-event register saga and post-booking signal waits); the read-only request pipeline runs in ordinary stateless services; router and policy are libraries/services the saga calls; pacing is a shared token-bucket service

**Summary:** EventRequest-level orchestration (parse → discover → rank → select → ≤3-attempt fall-through) is owned by a stateless RequestCoordinator driven by a transactional Postgres request-state row plus the FR-8.9 outbox — machinery the requirements already mandate — because every step above the register boundary is read-only and idempotent, and because FR-8.1's own workflowId (user:canonical_event_id) cannot exist until discovery/dedup has produced the canonical_event_id, so no proposal can literally run discover/rank inside the per-event workflow. Temporal carries exactly one workflow type, the per-(user,event) RegisterSaga (~10 activities, <300 LoC of deterministic code): policy-gate → lane branch (Meetup read-before-mutate API / browser detect-then-submit in a microVM / handoff task with a 7-day durable timer) → await_confirmation (timer+signal) → dedupe → calendar write → long-lived post-booking signal wait for reconcile/un-RSVP. The lane router is a pure, table-driven library evaluated twice (at selection for preference ordering, at the saga's policy gate against live automation_allowed); the kill-switch is a data-plane policy row checked per-activity AND re-checked pre-mutation inside the register/submit step — strictly stronger than a Temporal signal, which cannot interrupt an in-flight activity anyway. Idempotency keys are minted in deterministic workflow code (workflowId+runId+step; calendar key = base32hex(tenant+canonical_event_id) per FR-9.2); a Pacer service enforces (source,credential) token buckets with a deficit-round-robin per-app degrade mode carried as the G2 contingency. The payoff: the 300k/wk read-only pipeline never touches Temporal, the versioning-hostile workflow surface stays one small saga, and every binding safety property lives at the mutation boundary where it is actually enforced.

### Proposal 3

**Bias:** event-driven choreography — transactional outbox + broker as the spine between stages; Temporal scoped to small per-(user,event) side-effecting sagas; the event log IS the lifecycle

**Summary:** The system is an event-log spine, not a workflow monolith: intake, discovery, ranking, and selection are stateless services that each commit their state change plus an outbox event in one Postgres transaction; a relay publishes to a broker, and idempotent consumers (inbox-deduped) advance the pipeline. Temporal Cloud is deliberately scoped to the only place durable execution earns its cost — one small per-(user_id, canonical_event_id) workflow covering the side-effecting leg (resolve_membership → policy-gated register → confirmation wait → calendar write) plus the handoff-TTL branch. This shape structurally resolves the fork's central tension: FR-5.0's attempt loop runs at EventRequest granularity BEFORE a canonical_event_id is chosen, so it cannot live inside an FR-8.1 workflow keyed by canonical_event_id — a Selection Coordinator reducer owns it, advancing on RegistrationFailed events and starting at most 3 per-event workflows (at-least-once broker delivery × reject-duplicate workflowId = exactly-once start, no hand-rolled dedup). Policy and kill-switch are consumer-side gates evaluated inside every side-effecting activity against a Postgres policy store with ≤5s LISTEN/NOTIFY propagation, so engaging the kill-switch freezes every in-flight workflow at its next activity without enumerating or signaling a single workflow. The outbox that FR-8.9 mandates anyway is promoted from afterthought to spine: FR-7.3's audit log, the lifecycle projection, and notifications are all consumers of the same exactly-once event stream, and a Temporal Cloud or broker outage each degrades only its own half of the system.
