# ADR-006: Managed browser fleet (Browserbase) under a first-party sovereign trust plane

**Status:** accepted — with owner-ratification riders (see Consequences) · **Date:** 2026-07-10

## Context

The browser lane exists for exactly one launch source — Luma, browser-only and explicitly off the reliability SLA (FR-5.4, NFR-2) — yet it is the single place where a stored user credential meets an LLM-driven agent on a hostile web page, so it concentrates the system's hardest security obligations while carrying its loosest availability ones. The fork is who owns the browser fleet and where the trust boundary sits: a self-hosted fleet operated from day one, or a managed vendor fleet with every security-critical primitive kept first-party. The forces:

- **Scale is small at launch and modest at the horizon.** Peak demand is ~13.5 concurrent sessions at launch and ~135 at 100k users with ~90 sessions/min creation demand (NFR-4b's d15-derived Friday-peak basis); NFR-4b itself names "Scale 250+ or a self-hosted Chromium fleet at the ~100-concurrent self-host threshold" as co-equal branches. Self-host economic breakeven is ~5,000 concurrent — 37x the 100k design point.
- **Cost is a binding SLO.** NFR-5 caps the browser lane at ≤ $0.40 per confirmed RSVP; ~98% of lane cost is model tokens ($0.05–0.30), so fleet infrastructure must stay in the noise (~$0.0025/session managed vs $1.15–1.91/RSVP infra-only for day-one self-host at 1k users).
- **Security is enforceable-by-construction or it is nothing.** FR-2.2 (per-tenant DEK + encryption context), FR-2.5/2.6 (non-LLM broker, domain-pinning, zeroization, secret-free histories), FR-2.7/AC-13 (one ephemeral microVM per (user, event), egress allowlist), FR-2.4/AC-12 (cookie-first, same-worker-identity reuse), FR-5.7/5.8 (OTP as short-TTL secret via RelayInbox), FR-7.4/AC-52 (dual-LLM boundary), FR-5.5/AC-37 (detect-then-submit), FR-5.10/AC-41 (execution-time free-only guard), NFR-6 (CI secret-scan, AC-5/AC-6/AC-7 fixtures).
- **Failure must fail safe.** Saturation, vendor outage, ban, or injection detection terminates in a human-handoff task within NFR-2's handoff targets (p95 ≤ 60 s task, ≤ 120 s notified), never an autonomous-lane SLA breach (FR-6.4, AC-44/45, NFR-15).
- **Compliance forbids the classic self-host justifications.** No IP rotation or block circumvention ever (FR-7.6); datacenter egress IPs actively worsen the anti-bot friction that already caps browser write success at ~56–64% (O-4).
- **Preconditions.** Gate G3 (Luma validators accepting RelayInbox addresses) gates the entire FR-2.13 account-linking and OTP leg regardless of fleet ownership.

## Options considered

1. **Self-hosted Firecracker on EC2 bare metal from day one.** One snapshot-booted microVM per (user, event), per-node host-daemon broker (plaintext never crosses a network link), per-session nftables egress, deterministic worker-identity tuple. Rejected because it breaches NFR-5 for the entire launch window by its own arithmetic — infra-only $1.15–1.91 per browser RSVP at 1k users against the $0.40 bound, ~$3k/mo fixed for a ~13-session peak — and buys that breach with a headcount-shaped hypervisor/kernel/Chromium-CVE operations burden on a pre-PMF team, for an explicitly best-effort lane. Its decisive claim — that a managed vendor cannot satisfy FR-2.4 identity pinning or egress control — is refuted: Browserbase per-session proxy configuration routed through our own sticky-IP egress pins the (tenant, source) network identity, which is what the requirement actually demands. Its datacenter EIPs worsen anti-bot friction, and self-host breakeven sits at ~5,000 concurrent. Its security mechanics (pin-before-decrypt ordering, identity-tuple hash, label-payload AC-52 fixtures) are excellent and are grafted into the decision.

2. **Minimal-TCB capability broker on self-hosted gVisor from day one.** Concentrate every secret capability into one small formally-reviewable Rust broker (sole kms:Decrypt principal, CI-asserted); run everything else untrusted in runsc sandboxes (~150 ms starts, ~65 sessions per 64-vCPU host). Rejected for launch because it still pays day-one fleet ownership — gVisor+Chromium hardening spike, fleet ops, datacenter-IP CAPTCHA friction — for a 13-session peak a $0.0025/session vendor covers, and two load-bearing specifics fail verification: the 500–800-LOC broker claim cites exemplars carrying no LOC figures (a CDP+Playwright+grant-verify client realistically exceeds it, weakening the audit-500-lines economics), and its raw-screenshot Computer-Use pilot inside the action-proposal path trades the letter of binding AC-52. Its capability discipline is grafted nearly wholesale, and the fleet design is retained in full as the named self-host migration target.

3. **Managed vendor fleet (Browserbase) as written, unamended.** Rejected in its original form on a confirmed FR-2.6 violation: its OTP path signalled the plaintext code into Temporal workflow history (a secret per FR-5.8) while claiming FR-5.7/5.8 satisfied; it also left FR-2.4 identity reuse as a proxy-level convention rather than a deterministic broker check, and lacked pin-before-decrypt zero-release ordering, the single-decrypt-principal CI assertion, the screenshot lock, and a fail-closed per-session proxy canary. Every defect is fixed by grafts from options 1 and 2; the base topology stands. **This hardened composite is the decision.**

## Decision

We will run the browser fleet on Browserbase and keep every secret-touching primitive first-party in our VPC — a sovereign trust plane on a managed fleet.

- **Vault (FR-2.2, AC-5).** A siloed credential vault (dedicated Postgres, own subnet/IAM boundary) stores KMS-wrapped per-tenant DEKs and AES-256-GCM ciphertext with bound_origin, credential_type, and the capture-time worker-identity hash; {tenant_id, credential_type} encryption context is enforced by kms:EncryptionContext IAM condition keys; the vault role holds GenerateDataKey/Encrypt but NOT Decrypt. An ephemeral-secrets table holds KMS-encrypted OTP/magic-link rows with provider-TTL and a consumed-once flag.
- **Injection broker (FR-2.5/2.6, AC-6/AC-7).** A non-LLM broker is the ONLY IAM principal with kms:Decrypt on the credential CMK, asserted by a one-line CI policy test. Fixed fill order: live CDP origin check of top frame AND focused-element frame against bound_origin BEFORE any KMS call — a pin failure releases zero secret material — then decrypt into an mlock'd MADV_DONTDUMP buffer, Playwright fill over the session's CDP socket, zeroize buffer and DEK on every exit path, secret-free audit event. The broker refuses cookie injection unless the live identity-tuple hash matches the capture-time hash (FR-2.4 as a check, not a convention), and captures fresh post-login cookies via CDP directly into the vault.
- **Egress proxy and identity (FR-2.7, FR-2.4, AC-13 behavioral half).** All session traffic crosses our default-deny CONNECT/DNS proxy; the per-session allowlist is the event origin plus the source's first-party assets only. Egress IP slot = consistent-hash(tenant, source) from a fixed EIP pool plus a pinned fingerprint template; the tuple is hashed at cookie capture and re-verified before any reuse. A session-start egress canary requests a non-allowlisted host from inside every session and kills the session fail-closed if it is not blocked — converting vendor proxy enforcement from attestation into a per-session test.
- **Deterministic action gate and CaMeL split (FR-7.4/AC-52, FR-5.5, FR-5.10).** A non-LLM gate enforces a closed action grammar (click, fill-by-placeholder routed to the broker, allowlisted navigate, detect, submit, halt), rejects submit without a fresh NOT_PRESENT detect, runs the typed price==0 guard and policy/kill-switch evaluation before any submit, and holds the screenshot lock during credential fill. Raw DOM/screenshots reach only a tool-less Reader emitting schema-validated typed observations; the Planner's input schema has no field capable of carrying raw page text — CI-asserted, with label-borne payloads in the AC-52 fixtures.
- **OTP claim-check (FR-5.7/5.8, FR-8.4, FR-10.6, AC-38/AC-57/AC-75).** RelayInbox mail is deterministically extracted (sender-domain allowlist, no LLM), KMS-encrypted into the ephemeral-secrets table, and signalled to Temporal as {otp_ref, sender, expires_at} only; the broker alone redeems the ref, consumed-once, raced against a durable timer.
- **Fleet, governor, and exit (NFR-4b, FR-6.4).** One vendor Firecracker microVM per (user, event), created inside the register_or_rsvp activity and destroyed at activity end; keepAlive off, contexts/persistence hard-disabled by a wrapper plus CI assertion; nothing persists vendor-side. Developer tier (~13 concurrent) at launch, Startup, then Scale 250+ before the ~75k-user horizon. A pool governor admits at 90% of contracted concurrency with a token bucket on the creation cap and fails saturation synchronously into a pre-filled handoff task within NFR-2 targets. A vendor-agnostic Fleet Port (create_session(tenant, event_origin, proxy_config, identity_profile) → CDP endpoint) plus a tripwire at sustained 70 concurrent opens both the Scale contract motion and the gVisor self-host readiness spike; the named migration target is the option-2 gVisor design on standard EC2.
- **Launch parameters.** Attempt budget ≤ 3 (FR-5.0); 5-minute session wall-clock cap; governor watermark 90%; tripwire sustained 70 concurrent; OTP ref TTL = provider expiry; cookie-first with password fallback only after cookie rejection (AC-12); lane cost basis $0.15–0.40 per confirmed browser RSVP, within NFR-5 at every scale including day one.
- **Invariants (CI-enforced where mechanizable, NFR-6).** Exactly one decrypt principal; pin-before-decrypt; no LLM-bearing process has a channel that can return a secret (the broker types, it never returns plaintext); histories/prompts/logs carry only placeholders and opaque refs; one microVM per (user, event), destroyed at activity end; all traffic through the proxy, canary-verified per session; cookie injection only on identity-hash match; every browser-lane fault terminates in exactly one handoff task; submit only after fresh NOT_PRESENT; idempotency keys minted once in workflow state (FR-8.3).

## Consequences

**Easier:** NFR-5 is trivially satisfied at every scale (fleet infra 1–2% of lane cost); no hypervisor/kernel/Chromium-CVE operations at launch; NFR-4b scaling is a tier upgrade, not a build-out; residential-adjacent egress avoids self-inflicted datacenter-IP anti-bot friction; every security property the requirements gate on numerically — encryption context, sole-decrypt principal, pin-before-decrypt, egress deny, secret-free histories — is enforced in our infrastructure regardless of who boots the microVM, and the CI harness (AC-5/6/7, secret-scan, IAM assertion, egress canary) verifies it continuously.

**Harder:** vendor coupling — a Browserbase outage, price change, or ToS shift takes the browser lane down (bounded to off-SLA handoff degradation per FR-6.4, but Luma-lane availability is genuinely coupled to a Series-stage vendor); half of AC-13 (kernel separation) is vendor-attested rather than first-party-proven until migration; fill-time plaintext transits the vendor CDP gateway inside TLS — an incident class self-hosting would not have, bounded because cookie-first means the transported secret is usually a revocable short-TTL cookie and nothing persists vendor-side; proxy unbypassability ultimately rests on vendor network enforcement, with the fail-closed canary as the test and the kill-switch (FR-7.2) as the response to a discovered bypass; the central broker is a fill-path single point of failure, run as an HA pair failing closed to handoff (the Meetup lane has no broker dependency); DBSC/device-binding adoption by Luma would reject all cookie replay from ephemeral VMs, pushing sessions to fresh OTP login (monitored via cookie-rejection rate, O-7).

**Follow-ups committed:** Fleet Port implemented from day one so the migration is an adapter swap; dual-track tripwire response at sustained 70 concurrent (Scale contract motion + gVisor readiness spike, final call at ~100 concurrent with measured success rates, durations, and terms); pre-migration load test calibrating the ~0.9 vCPU/session and ~65-sessions/host assumptions; identity-decay runbook (proactively expire cookies on EIP or fingerprint-template change); anomalous-cookie-use auto-revoke; G3 verification before design freeze.

**Owner-ratification riders:**
- OWNER RATIFY: the AC-13 kernel-separation half is verified by vendor attestation (SOC 2, contractual per-session Firecracker isolation, per-session API evidence) plus behavioral tests until self-host migration, at which point AC-13 becomes fully first-party — the decision's one explicit binding-constraint exception.
- OWNER RATIFY: fill-time plaintext transit through the vendor CDP gateway as the accepted launch trade (per-fill exposure, mostly short-TTL revocable cookies, immediate zeroization, no vendor persistence; re-examined at the tripwire).
- OWNER RATIFY: the tripwire value of sustained 70 concurrent (≈70% of Startup's 100) as the trigger opening both the Scale 250+ contract motion and the gVisor readiness spike, and the dual-track spend it implies.
- OWNER RATIFY: the interpretation of AC-13's "kernel" — if a gVisor userspace kernel does not qualify and hardware virtualization per session is required, the migration config swaps to runsc -platform=kvm at roughly double the host count/cost; rule now so migration sizing is decided consciously.
- OWNER RATIFY: the Browserbase Scale contract checklist to lock before ~75k users — ≥250 concurrency, 150/min creation, ZDR/data-handling posture, contractual per-session isolation, proxy-enforcement guarantee, persistence-off guarantee — with a regression on any item firing the self-host trigger early.
- OWNER RATIFY: opting out of Anthropic's computer-use injection classifier (whose flagged-action human confirmation breaks the no-per-action-confirmation scope), substituting the CaMeL Reader/Planner split plus the deterministic gates as the structural control.
