# ADR-008: Central organizer-change detection keyed by distinct watched events (reconciles the Panel 1 / Panel 4 conflict)

**Status:** accepted — with owner-ratification riders (see Consequences) · **Date:** 2026-07-10

## Context

FR-8.7a requires that for every (user, event) in `registered`/`scheduled` state, an organizer cancel or reschedule is detected — via webhook where a source offers one, otherwise by scheduled re-poll — normalized to schema.org `eventStatus`, and fed into the FR-8.7 reconcile branch; NFR-17 binds the poll path to ≤6h detection-to-reconcile (owner target). No launch source offers an attendee-facing webhook, so every lane is poll- or email-driven, and the detection topology is a quota-arithmetic problem before it is a software-design problem. The forces:

- **Ticketmaster:** one shared app key, 5,000 calls/day + 5 rps (FR-10.4). The catalog crawl already consumes ~2,000 calls/day typical with a soft cap of 4,500 and a 500-call reserve; under budget pressure the ADR-002 degrade ladder demotes the 15–90d window to 12h then 24h cadence. Any detection scheme that rides only the crawl inherits that demotion: a watched event starting >48h out in a demoted cell would be detected in 12–24h, breaching NFR-17.
- **Meetup:** 500 points/60s whose per-token vs per-app scoping is UNCONFIRMED (design-blocking spike G2, requirements §8); the ~5 pts/status-query cost figure appears in no dossier and is an assumption. All Meetup traffic must flow through the FR-10.4 fair-share pacer keyed (source, credential).
- **Luma and handoff-lane events** (registered outside our surface): no API path at all; only the public event page's JSON-LD Event block (FR-3.7 ACL) and RelayInbox organizer change-emails, the latter gated on G3 relay-address acceptance.
- **Scale:** at 100k users the naive per-workflow poll loop costs ~96M Temporal actions/month at 200k watched events and multiplies source fetches N-watchers-fold per popular event — against exactly the two quotas above.
- **Correctness bindings:** AC-60 (change detected within NFR-17 cadence via each source's declared modality), AC-61 (cancel removes the calendar entry and notifies), AC-21 (a second tenant adds zero live TM calls), NFR-15/FR-6.4 (handoff-lane volume must not pollute autonomous-lane SLA math), and gate G1 (unmeasured autonomous/handoff mix) which leaves all watched-population sizing assumption-based.

Two panel-decided mechanisms conflicted on the Ticketmaster feeder and this ADR records the reconciliation: the discovery-topology decision specified a full-population by-id re-poll of every watched TM event every 6h from the 500-call reserve, while the handoff-lifecycle decision specified crawl-delta plus a targeted by-id refresh only for watched events starting <48h, capped at 500 calls/day. The rubric-gate consistency adversary surfaced the conflict during design integration.

## Options considered

1. **Per-workflow poll timers (each per-(user,event) Temporal workflow runs its own 6h durable-timer poll loop).** Keeps detection inside the execution spine with no separate service, and the engine's timers and retries come free. Rejected because its costs scale with user-event pairs, not events: ~96M Temporal Cloud actions/month at 200k watched events by the proposal's own arithmetic, and N watchers of one popular event issue N duplicate source fetches per cycle — against a Meetup quota whose per-token sharding is forum-sourced and unconfirmed (G2) and a TM key budget of 5,000/day. The proposal's own documented G2 contingency was a central poller; deciding that now avoids paying to migrate to it later.

2. **Central detection with TM covered by crawl-delta plus a <48h-only targeted refresh (the handoff-lifecycle panel's original TM feeder).** Central and canonical-event-keyed, and cheapest on the TM budget: the crawl delta costs zero extra calls and the by-id refresh spends the reserve only on imminent events. Rejected as stated because it silently couples booked-event detection to crawl cadence: under the ADR-002 degrade ladder a watched event starting more than 48h out sits in a cell that may have been demoted to 12–24h cadence, so its cancel/reschedule is detected late and NFR-17 fails for exactly the population NFR-17 scopes. The <48h idea is not discarded — it survives as the priority order within the reserve (soonest-starting events re-polled first), not as an eligibility cap.

3. **Central change-detection service keyed by distinct watched canonical events, with TM covered by crawl-delta PLUS a full-population by-id re-poll of every watched TM event every 6h from the 500-call reserve (chosen).** Detection cost scales with distinct watched events — one poll covers all subscribers of a popular event — and the TM feeder is the only variant under which NFR-17 survives the degrade ladder, because every registered/scheduled TM event is re-polled by id on its own 6h cycle independent of any cell demotion. Cost: the reserve bounds coverage at ~125 concurrently watched TM events (500 calls/day ÷ 4 polls/day), which is a sizing assumption until G1 measures the request mix — carried as an owner-ratification rider with an explicit overflow posture rather than treated as a defect.

4. **Event-sourced DiffService over an append-only lifecycle log (detection deltas appended to canonical-event streams, consumers fan out).** Its detection topology — canonical-event-keyed, normalized eventStatus deltas — is the best-shaped of the alternatives and is grafted into the decision. Rejected as a whole because the log-as-source-of-truth substrate it rides on buys nothing the requirements don't already provide more cheaply (FR-7.3 audit log, FR-8.9 outbox, NFR-13 backups) while imposing standing engineering taxes: append-before-act bracketing, single-writer grant enforcement, snapshot-xip offset cursors, and a second replay semantic coexisting with Temporal's own history replay.

## Decision

We will run ONE central change-detection service, keyed by distinct watched canonical events, feeding the FR-8.7 reconcile branch via Temporal signals.

- **Watch registry.** A `watch_registry` of distinct (canonical_event_id, source) rows, maintained by a transactional-outbox consumer on lifecycle entry to and exit from `registered`/`scheduled`. Detection cost therefore scales with distinct watched events, never with user-event pairs; terminal transitions remove watch interest in the same release step, proven by the nightly invariant query (zero watch rows for terminal lifecycle rows).
- **Ticketmaster feeder (Panel 1 canonical — records the panel reconciliation).** Two layers: (a) the catalog crawl's hash-delta comparator on eventStatus/startDate/venue against stored canonical rows — zero extra API calls; (b) a by-id GET of EVERY watched TM event every 6h, drawn from the 500-call daily reserve. Layer (b) is what makes NFR-17 independent of the ADR-002 degrade ladder: booked-event detection never depends on crawl cell cadence. Within the reserve, re-polls are prioritized soonest-starting-first — the surviving form of the <48h refinement, a priority order rather than a cap. The crawler and this re-poller remain the only code paths linked against the TM client, so AC-21 holds by construction.
- **Meetup feeder.** A 3h poller issuing ONE status query per distinct watched event, round-robin across the watchers' own OAuth tokens through the FR-10.4 fair-share pacer (batched event-id queries where the G2 spike confirms point economics). On consumer revocation or auth error the feeder circuit-opens and degrades to public event-page JSON-LD polling — detection survives the revocation that kills the RSVP lane.
- **Luma + handoff-lane feeder.** A 3h unauthenticated GET of the public event page, parsing the JSON-LD Event block through the FR-3.7 ACL, globally self-capped at ~1 rps human cadence; RelayInbox organizer change-emails, extracted by the same allowlisted per-source extractors as completion matching, act as a near-real-time accelerator (gated on G3).
- **Normalization + dedup.** Every feeder normalizes to schema.org `eventStatus` plus new start/end into an `event_change` table with a UNIQUE dedup fingerprint hash(event_id + status + dates), so a change caught by both the 3h poll and a relay email inserts once; workflows additionally no-op redelivered signals on the fingerprint held in workflow state.
- **Fanout.** A fanout worker joins `event_change` rows against affected lifecycle rows and signals each (user, event) workflow `organizer_change{fingerprint, …}`; the workflow executes the reconcile as journaled activities (cancel → delete calendar entry by deterministic id + notify + terminal `cancelled`; reschedule → freeBusy re-check over the new window, patch start/end/timeZone, notify → `reconciled`), satisfying AC-60/AC-61. A fanout signal hitting a just-closed workflow is recorded on the change row and repaired by the 15-min sweeper via idempotent CalendarPort calls.
- **Observability.** Per-(source, watched-event) staleness gauges alarm at the 6h NFR-17 bound; N consecutive poll failures flip the gauge to alarm while the calendar entry is kept (stale beats wrongly-deleted). Coverage loss is alerted, never silent.
- **Launch parameters:** poll cadence 3h for Meetup/Luma/handoff-lane (one-missed-cycle headroom inside ≤6h); TM re-poll cycle 6h from the 500-call reserve (~125 watched-event capacity); Luma/handoff global self-cap ~1 rps; sweeper 15 min + 5 min grace.

## Consequences

Easier: NFR-17 becomes provable per source with a single measurement point (inject a source-side change, assert the reconcile fires within cadence — AC-60) instead of N per-workflow timers; popular events cost one poll regardless of watcher count, so quota exposure scales with the distinct-event corpus; the degrade ladder can shed far-window crawl freshness without ever touching booked-event detection; Temporal action spend drops from ~96M/month to the fanout worker's signal volume; duplicate detections converge structurally on the fingerprint.

Harder / accepted risks: the detection service is a new always-on subsystem with its own registry-consumer, three feeders, and fanout worker to operate — a failure here stalls all reconciles, mitigated by the staleness gauges and the sweeper repair path. The TM reserve is a hard coverage ceiling (~125 concurrently watched TM events at 6h) sized on an unmeasured request mix (G1). Meetup detection economics rest on an unpublished point-cost assumption (G2). Handoff-lane users who register with their real email instead of the relay prefill silence the change-email accelerator, leaving page-poll-only detection — and an invite-gated page (403) reduces that to nothing; such tasks are flagged poll-only on the reconcile SLO dashboard. Public pages dropping their JSON-LD block degrade handoff-lane detection until the fetcher is patched — surfaced within one 3h tick, but the window is real.

Follow-ups committed: G2 spike measures Meetup quota scoping and per-status-query point cost before any Meetup-lane SLA is set; G1 request-mix measurement recalibrates the TM reserve; RelayInbox acceptance (G3) instrumented from day one; recorded-fixture contract tests per source page schema; nightly invariant query on watch-registry hygiene.

- **Owner-ratification riders:**
  - OWNER RATIFY: 3h poll cadence for the Meetup and Luma/handoff-lane feeders (one-missed-cycle headroom inside the ≤6h NFR-17 target).
  - OWNER RATIFY: TM re-poll reserve sizing — 500 calls/day supports ~125 concurrently watched TM events at 6h cadence; overflow prioritizes soonest-starting events, alerts, and falls back to crawl delta for the remainder. Confirm after G1's request-mix measurement lands.
  - OWNER RATIFY: the ~5 pts per Meetup status query figure is an ASSUMPTION the G2 spike must measure; under the per-app worst case at 100k users the watched-event poll load could exceed the quota ceiling — the carried mitigations are per-token sharding (if the spike confirms it), adaptive cadence above the 6h floor for far-future events, and explicit coverage-loss alerting rather than silent staleness.
