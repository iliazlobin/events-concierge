# Adjacent "Do-It-For-Me" Concierge & Scheduling Products — Trust and UX Patterns

> This dimension supplies the empirical trust/pricing/autonomy priors — a decade of concierge deaths, live scheduling agents, and 2026 consumer surveys — that our Events Concierge's autonomy defaults, pricing, and positioning must be calibrated against.

## Landscape inventory

### Human-labor concierges (the graveyard, plus one survivor)

- **Yohana (Panasonic)** (dead; https://panasonic.jp/subscription/products/yohana.html) — Human "family concierge" subscription (Guide + Specialists + vetted pro network + app) taking delegated household tasks — research, bookings, planning — for busy families; founded by Yoky Matsuoka. $249/mo standard (promo $149/mo). US service ended Sept 30 2025, Japan membership ended Jan 30 2026, Panasonic Well division dissolved Mar 31 2026; Panasonic's CFO said it never reached "clear scaling and monetization." The flagship cautionary tale: even with a patient corporate owner, a human-labor concierge at a consumer price point could not scale. Validates that our concierge must be pure software with handoff-to-the-USER, never handoff-to-paid-ops-staff.
- **Duckbill** (active, verified Feb 2026; https://getduckbill.com/pricing) — AI + human "copilots" personal concierge: appointment scheduling, research, household admin, gifting. AI drafts, humans verify and execute; human bandwidth strictly metered per plan, with task cost shown BEFORE submission. Roughly $99/mo Individual, $169/mo Household (pricing has since widened to ~$49–350/mo across four tiers). $33M seed+Series A led by Forerunner (Greycroft, Inspired, General Catalyst); founded 2022 by Meghan Joyce (ex-Oppenheimer/Uber). Best-in-class consent UX in the human-hybrid tier: upfront clarifying questions + cost preview before commit ("no surprises"). Its price band is the ceiling anchor for concierge-with-humans; a software-only events concierge must land far below it.
- **Magic (getmagic.com)** (pivoted; https://getmagic.com/pricing/) — Consumer text-a-task concierge (2015) turned B2B virtual-EA staffing agency: dedicated human EAs ("AI-supercharged" with LLM tooling) for SMBs, 24/7 on-demand. Magic 24/7 $199/week (~$35/hr per task-hour); part-time $270/wk; full-time $540/wk. Survived a decade only by abandoning consumer pricing for B2B staffing economics. Second confirmation of the Yohana/Fin lesson: human-in-the-loop execution only pencils at B2B rates.
- **Fin (Sam Lessin / Andrew Kortina)** (pivoted 2019; https://techcrunch.com/2017/12/22/fin-assistant/) — 24/7 human-operator assistant (research, scheduling, commerce, calls) with encrypted "Vault" of stored payment/preference data, itemized per-minute billing, thumbs up/down charge disputes. $1/minute (switched from $120/mo subscription because users arbitraged it). Well-funded and high-profile; consumer business abandoned as capital-intensive. Lessin's postmortem is the sharpest economics lesson in the corpus: error rates kill agent margins and the long tail of almost-right tasks compounds at scale — directly predicts why our browser-automation RSVP lane must be best-effort with cheap human handoff, not an SLA.
- **Clara Labs** (original human-QA service dead; brand relaunched as pure software; https://www.claralabs.com/) — Human-verified AI email scheduling ("cyborg" model), premium positioning, ~$99–399/mo while alive. YC-backed and loved by early users; the human-QA version shut down on ops cost and single-use-case narrowness. Note: claralabs.com is live in 2026 as "Clara AI, Inc." selling a scheduling agent explicitly advertising zero humans in the loop — the humans died, the software survived. Human-in-the-loop as a QUALITY layer is now redundant at LLM accuracy; the modern equivalent is confirm-taps by the user. Also: a single-verb product (schedule) was too narrow; our loop spans discover→RSVP→calendar→reconcile.

### Software-margin scheduling/calendar agents (the live market)

- **Howie** (active; https://howie.com/) — AI "calendar secretary" you CC on email threads; negotiates times, books, reschedules; deliberately impersonates the human-EA delegation ritual (email, name, signature). $25–95/mo tiers (+$20/mo custom assistant email); the $95 tier adds a 24/7 human-EA backup team. $6M from Sequoia + a16z at $30M valuation; ~1,000 paying customers out of beta. Proof that consumers/prosumers pay $25–95/mo for an agent that ACTS on their calendar when the delegation metaphor is familiar. Positioning insight: Howie "owns delegation" rather than selling AI — same move as our "concierge does 95%, you tap once."
- **Skej** (active; https://skej.com/pricing) — Email-CC AI scheduling assistant (Google/Outlook/Zoom/Teams); finds times, books, reschedules. Free tier + Pro ~$10–15/user/mo; small, competes on price under Howie. Marks the price floor of the acting-scheduler market; together with Howie it brackets the $10–95/mo consumer willingness band for software that writes to your calendar.
- **Reclaim.ai (Dropbox)** (acquired Aug 2024, product alive, ~23 employees May 2026; https://reclaim.ai/blog/dropbox-acquires-reclaim) — AI calendar optimizer: habits, focus time, task sync; deliberately "gentle" autonomy — blocks flexible time and suggests, rarely forces moves. Starter $8/user/mo, Business $12. The "gentle autonomy" pole: earns trust by making every automated block visibly movable. Also evidence that single-feature calendar tools exit via acquisition rather than standing alone.
- **Clockwise** (dead Mar 27 2026; https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47402579) — Team-wide meeting-optimization AI; auto-rearranged meetings to create focus blocks. Freemium + per-seat SaaS. $76M raised; 23M meetings optimized, 8M focus-time hours, thousands of enterprise customers — still not viable standalone: Salesforce acqui-hired the team into Agentforce and killed the product with ~1 week migration notice, data deleted. Even heavy traction in calendar-writing autonomy didn't sustain a standalone company; value pooled to platforms. Supports building a full closed loop (discover→act→reconcile) rather than one automated calendar feature.
- **Motion** (active; https://get-alfred.ai/blog/motion-pricing) — Aggressive auto-scheduler: AI continuously rearranges your calendar around task priorities and deadlines; positions as tool consolidation. Pro $19/mo ($12.73 annual), Business $29/seat/mo. Large prosumer base; persistent complaints about over-aggressive rescheduling requiring manual oversight. The "aggressive autonomy" pole. The Motion-vs-Reclaim contrast is the cleanest live market comparison on autonomy defaults: aggressive sells to power users but generates trust complaints; gentle retains broader users. Our default should be Reclaim-style (suggest/digest) with opt-in escalation.
- **Lindy** (active; https://www.lindy.ai/pricing) — General AI-EA/agent platform; meeting scheduler checks availability, handles back-and-forth, books, syncs calendar. Explicit confirmation-only mode until you trust the flow end-to-end, with condition branches deciding when the agent may book directly vs must ask. Free tier; Plus $49.99/mo (credit-metered); Enterprise custom. Well-funded (Flo Crivello); prominent in 2026 AI-EA roundups. The clearest productized "graduated autonomy" pattern to copy: start every lane in confirm-first mode, promote lanes to autonomous after observed correct behavior. Maps 1:1 onto our per-source honest-split lanes.
- **x.ai (Amy/Andrew Ingram)** (dead 2021, sunset after Bizzabo acqui-hire; https://www.usecarly.com/blog/calendly-vs-xai/) — First-wave fully-automated email scheduling agent (CC amy@x.ai); ~$93M raised; free → $17–39/mo tiers. Huge hype 2014–2018; secretly heavy human annotation behind the automation; never reached sustainable economics. Pre-LLM proof that the CC-an-agent UX resonates AND that overpromising full autonomy while humans mop up errors destroys margins. The 2025–26 cohort (Howie/Skej) is the same product finally feasible — timing risk for us is now low on NLU, still high on cross-site actuation.

### Travel/vertical agentic booking (where autonomy is actually shipping)

- **Mindtrip (+ Sabre Mosaic + PayPal)** (active; launched "Mindtrip Flights" agentic booking May 6 2026; https://www.sabre.com/resources/newsroom/mindtrip-launches-travels-first-all-in-one-agentic-ai-flight-booking-experience-powered-by-partnership-with-sabre-and-paypal/) — AI travel planner that completes flight search→compare→BOOK→pay entirely in chat ("intent to transaction in one conversation") via Sabre GDS inventory and PayPal agentic-commerce rails. Consumer-free; monetizes booking/affiliate rails; VC-backed first-mover. Note HOW they de-risked autonomy: licensed inventory API + trusted payment intermediary + user confirms payment in-flow. Autonomy came from supply-side partnerships, not browser automation — same shape as our API-first connector strategy.
- **Layla.ai** (active; https://layla.ai/) — Free consumer AI travel agent: itineraries, live flight/hotel pricing, PriceLock; primarily hands off to booking partners rather than completing checkout. Free (affiliate monetization); "trusted by millions" claim, strong consumer brand. The dominant consumer pattern in 2026 travel AI is still recommend-then-handoff — the market leader ships our "one-tap human handoff" lane as its ONLY lane. Our honest split is ahead of, not behind, consumer norms.
- **Expedia agentic AI program (B2B MCP server)** (active, announced 2026, shipping "within months"; https://www.geekwire.com/2026/were-experimenting-aggressively-how-expedia-sees-ai-reshaping-travel-and-its-own-business/) — MCP server giving partner AI agents direct live-inventory access for autonomous search/book; consumer-side agent experiments (rebooking on disruption, dynamic packaging). B2B pricing. 80% of travel executives plan agent deployment at scale (Skift) while consumer willingness for fully autonomous booking is ~2%. Incumbents are making AUTONOMOUS ACTION a supply-side capability (MCP/API), which over time converts our browser-automation fallback lanes into API lanes.
- **Alaska Airlines "Alaska Inspires"** (active; https://www.microsoft.com/en/customers/story/25850-alaska-airlines-azure-openai) — NL destination/flight discovery (Azure OpenAI): "where can I see live music this summer?" → bookable options; booking remains user-completed; agentic booking only "under careful look." Free feature; 7.16% conversion, 75% planning-time reduction, 90% satisfaction. NL discovery alone measurably converts — and a major airline still won't ship autonomous booking in 2026.

### Platform incumbents commoditizing the "acting" layer

- **Google "Ask for Me" / Gemini agentic booking** (active; rolled out 2025, expanded at I/O May 2026 to home repair, beauty, pet care US-wide; https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/) — Google's AI phones businesses on your behalf to collect pricing/availability, returns a digest by text/email in ~30 min; agentic booking in Search/Gemini for select verticals. Free, with default distribution to the entire US search population. Two lessons: (1) Google's chosen trust UX is exactly digest-then-user-decides; (2) platform giants will commoditize single-vertical "agent calls/books for you." Our defensible layer is the personal taste model + standing weekly intent + calendar reconciliation, not the phone call/RSVP itself.
- **OpenTable Concierge (+ Agentforce back-of-house)** (active, launched Aug 2025; https://www.opentable.com/restaurant-solutions/resources/meet-concierge-your-new-ai-powered-booking-advantage/) — Gen-AI Q&A assistant embedded in 60k+ restaurant profiles (answers ~80% of diner questions); conversational booking on the roadmap. Free to diners; restaurant SaaS side. Source platforms are building their own concierges that will prefer first-party agents and may treat third-party actuation as hostile — reinforces ToS-tier caution and the value of acting strictly as the authenticated user.
- **OpenAI ChatGPT Agent / Operator** (active, general-purpose; https://medium.com/utopian/chatgpt-agent-is-a-nightmare-c2f04783c10e) — General browser-driving agent that can shop, fill forms, book; requires user takeover for logins/payments. Bundled in ChatGPT paid tiers ($20–200/mo). Massive distribution, but launch reception (July 2025) was notably hostile on trusted actions ("no chance I'm going to entrust something to go off and buy shit… for me"); Altman himself warned against high-stakes use. The generalists create the category's expectations AND its trust scar tissue. A vertical concierge wins by being reliable in ONE domain with domain-specific guardrails (per-source lanes, reconciliation) where the generalist is erratic everywhere.

### Reservation-access economy and its regulation

- **Appointment Trader** (active but banned in NY under the Restaurant Reservation Anti-Piracy Act; relaunching in NYC with an AI-chat interface hoping for Section 230 cover; https://columbianewsservice.com/2025/07/28/new-york-banned-reservation-resales-now-appointment-trader-is-testing-the-law-with-ai/) — Marketplace for buying/selling reservation slots, fed largely by bot-sniped Resy/OpenTable bookings; 20–30% take rate; ~$7M reservation sales 2024, ~50k users. Provoked the NY law plus copycat bills in CA/FL/IL/LA and two more states; Resy data shows presumed-bot reservations no-show 4x and late-cancel 2x vs normal users. Defines the regulatory/PR minefield for automated RSVP: the legislated harm is bot bookings with no genuine intent. Our concierge must be visibly the opposite — authenticated user, genuine intent, aggressive auto-un-RSVP hygiene — or it inherits scalper optics on capacity-limited free events too.
- **Dorsia** (active, growing; https://www.dorsia.com/) — Members-club reservation app: guaranteed tables in exchange for prepaid per-person minimum spend committed at booking. Basic $200/yr; Premium $5k/yr (with $4k credits); Premium Plus $25k/yr. ~30,000 paying members; $100–200k daily revenue (CNN, Mar 2026). Commitment-at-booking as a trust mechanism aligning user, platform, and venue — the paid mirror of RSVP-with-genuine-intent. Also evidence people pay real money for guaranteed ACCESS, not discovery; a future paid tier could sell reliability of getting in, not recommendations.

### Survey corpus

- **ACI Worldwide + Accenture + Bain + Harris trust studies** (papers, 2025–2026; https://www.bain.com/insights/agentic-ai-commerce-hinges-on-consumer-trust/) — Cross-industry measurements of consumer willingness to delegate action/payment to AI agents; Accenture n=25,590 across 16 countries. Quantifies the trust cliff our honest-split framing is built on — see F1/F2 below.

## Verified findings

**F1 — The trust cliff at payment/full autonomy is precisely quantified in 2026: consumers embrace delegated RESEARCH and bounded ACTION but reject autonomous PAYMENT by roughly an order of magnitude.** [confirmed]
Accenture Consumer Pulse 2026 (n=25,590, 16 countries): 74% would delegate routine agent tasks and even trust an agent over a friend for a purchase, 32% would let an agent decide WITHIN set budget/brand parameters, but only 9% accept fully autonomous agent-led payment (fact-check: cite 9%, not "9–12%"). Bain: 72% have used AI, 73% open to AI research help, only 24% comfortable letting AI complete a purchase, 10% have actually done it; trust concentrates in payment intermediaries (PayPal/Apple Pay) over platforms. Skift (Mar 2026): ~2% of US consumers willing to use fully autonomous travel booking while 80% of travel executives plan to deploy such agents. Harris (Feb 2026): 39% trust AI for everyday purchases. Consistent shape: delegation-within-parameters is mainstream; unsupervised commit of money/identity is fringe (74% → 9% is ~8x).

**F2 — Agent mistakes are near-unforgivable in consumer trust terms; reconciliation/undo is a retention feature, not polish.** [adjusted]
ACI Worldwide's June 2026 YouGov survey (2,080 UK adults): six in ten UK consumers would stop using an AI shopping agent after ONE mistake (also: only 19% trust AI for everyday purchases vs 55% for a human adviser; ACI recommends explicit approvals, spending limits, accountability). ChatGPT Agent's launch backlash centered on exactly this trust gap. The supply-side mirror is Sam Lessin's Fin postmortem — his 2023 "Lessons of Fin for This Generation of 'AI Assistants'" thread (not the 2017 TechCrunch launch profile, which contains none of this) — arguing error rates and the long tail of almost-right cases kill assistant economics. Caveat: the ACI figure is stated intent about shopping/payment agents; applying it to wrong-RSVP/calendar writes is extrapolation, but the direction is well supported. Implication: wrong-RSVP/wrong-calendar-write rate is THE product metric; silent-failure detection, visible receipts, and instant undo (un-RSVP) bound the damage of inevitable errors.

**F3 — Every human-labor concierge at consumer prices has died, fled the segment, or shed its humans; survivors are software-margin agents ($10–95/mo) or B2B staffing (~$35/hr).** [adjusted]
Yohana ($249/mo, Panasonic-funded) shut US Sept 30 2025 / Japan Jan 30 2026, division dissolved Mar 31 2026 — CFO cited no path to scaling/monetization; Panasonic's successor "Umi" drops the human-concierge model. Fin ($1/min, moved off $120/mo because users arbitraged it) exited consumer in 2019 on unit economics. Clara's original human-QA scheduling service died on ops cost — the brand now lives on as a pure-software agent explicitly advertising zero humans in the loop. Magic survives only as B2B hourly staffing. Duckbill ($49–350/mo across 4 tiers, $33M raised) is the lone consumer hybrid still standing, and only by strictly metering human bandwidth (task allocations + paid boosts, not "credits"). Pure-software actors (Howie $25–95/mo — whose top tier itself adds a human-EA backup — Skej, Lindy, Motion, Reclaim) are the growth segment. Our "concierge does 95%, you tap once" is exactly the cost structure the graveyard prescribes: the only affordable human is the user.

**F4 — A canonical, convergent autonomy-UX pattern kit now exists across acting-agent products.** [adjusted]
Six patterns, all verified in shipping products or codified pattern literature: (1) GRADUATED AUTONOMY — Lindy's per-action "Ask for Confirmation" toggle + Safe Mode (per docs.lindy.ai, not its blog): start supervised, loosen as trust builds; (2) UPFRONT POLICY BUDGETS — the Accenture 32% delegate-within-budget/brand segment; Stripe Issuing-for-agents spend controls (live API caps by amount, merchant category, vendor — card-level controls, not "signed mandates"); (3) PREVIEW BEFORE COMMIT — Duckbill shows task cost before submission; (4) DIGEST-THEN-USER-DECIDES — Google Ask for Me phones businesses, returns an aggregated comparison, the user commits; (5) RECEIPTS/LOGS/UNDO — agentic-UX pattern literature (AI UX Playground; Smashing Magazine Feb 2026) converges on every action producing a receipt with rollback; (6) AUTO-DEMOTION to confirm-mode when risk crosses a threshold (money, novelty, new vendor). Motion-vs-Reclaim illustrates aggressive-vs-gentle default positioning as a market contrast — not a literal A/B test — and the gentle default wins broad trust; aggressive is an opt-in power mode.

**F5 — Pricing anchors.** (researcher confidence: high)
Consumers pay $10–30/mo for software that acts on their calendar (Reclaim $8–12, Skej $10–15, Motion $19–29), $25–95/mo when it credibly replaces an assistant's delegation loop (Howie $25–95, Lindy $49.99), $99–249/mo only with humans — a dying tier (Duckbill alive, Yohana dead, Magic B2B) — and premium money flows to guaranteed ACCESS (Dorsia $200–$25k/yr, 30k members, $100–200k/day revenue) rather than better recommendations. Howie's 1,000 paying customers at launch and $6M round at just $30M valuation shows a real-but-modest consumer segment. Recommendation-only products (Layla, Alaska Inspires) are free/affiliate — nobody pays for discovery alone.

**F6 — Automated third-party booking is becoming a regulated/hostile category where the platform sees no genuine attendance intent.** (researcher confidence: high)
NY's Restaurant Reservation Anti-Piracy Act (2024) plus copycat bills in 6+ states target bot-made bookings, justified by Resy data: bot reservations no-show 4x and late-cancel 2x. Appointment Trader ($7M GMV, 50k users, 20–30% take) was banned in NY and is testing an AI-chat/Section-230 workaround. Free events with caps (popular Meetups, Luma parties, Partiful) have identical dynamics — organizers already fight RSVP-and-flake. An RSVP agent that books speculatively and never cleans up is indistinguishable from the villains of this legislation; one that cancels promptly when plans change is actively BETTER than a human flake.

**F7 — Platform incumbents are absorbing the "agent that acts" layer per-vertical, so booking/RSVP itself will commoditize; durable differentiation is cross-source aggregation + persistent personal taste + calendar reconciliation.** [unverifiable — researcher confidence: medium]
Google phones businesses and is expanding agentic booking verticals to all US users free; OpenTable ships a first-party concierge across 60k restaurants; Expedia exposes live inventory to any partner agent via MCP; Mindtrip completes flight checkout in-chat via licensed rails. None of these crosses SOURCES on the user's behalf with a standing brief ("keep my Friday evenings interesting") or reconciles resulting commitments on a calendar over time. Notably, when incumbents ship autonomy they de-risk via licensed APIs + payment intermediaries — mirroring our API-first-plus-fallback architecture.

**F8 — Single-feature calendar-automation companies do not survive standalone even with strong traction.** (researcher confidence: high)
Clockwise ($76M raised, 23M meetings optimized) acqui-hired by Salesforce and killed in one week (Mar 27 2026, accounts deleted, no read-only mode — a distrust event for the whole category); Reclaim sold to Dropbox (2024); x.ai sunset (2021); Clara's human-QA service wound down. A consumer events concierge should own an end-to-end loop (standing intent → discovery → action → reconciled calendar → taste memory) whose data compounds, rather than a single automated verb.

**F9 — The winning delegation FRAMING borrows a familiar human ritual rather than advertising "AI autonomy."** (researcher confidence: medium)
CC-the-assistant (Howie/Skej/x.ai), a named secretary persona, or digest-in-your-inbox (Google); autonomy is granted per-lane after observed competence. Howie explicitly "owns delegation" — users describe hiring a secretary, not enabling an AI feature. Lindy onboards in confirmation-only mode. Fin front-loaded trust with an onboarding survey + encrypted Vault so delegation felt like briefing an assistant once, then approving exceptions. Nobody successful asks for blanket autonomy upfront. For us: the NL standing brief ("Friday evenings, after work, nothing over $0") IS the onboarding survey, and each source/lane should carry its own earned-autonomy state.

**F10 — NL discovery+ranking alone has independently measured conversion value even with zero autonomy.** (researcher confidence: medium)
Alaska Inspires converts at 7.16%, cuts planning time 75%, 90% satisfaction. This de-risks our always-autonomous lanes (discovery, ranking, calendar) as standalone value. It also matches the market's free-tier shape (Layla, Mindtrip planning, OpenTable Concierge all free at the discovery layer) — discovery cannot be the paid feature; ACTING and RECONCILING must be.

## Product implications

1. **Launch scope validated — keep it.** Free-RSVP-only sits below the quantified trust cliff (32% accept bounded delegation vs 9% autonomous payment). When paid checkout eventually ships: per-transaction confirm + budget mandate + trusted payment intermediary (PayPal/Apple Pay-style), never stored-card autonomy.
2. **Adopt the six-pattern autonomy kit as explicit requirements:** (1) per-lane graduated autonomy starting confirm-first with promotion after N correct actions (Lindy); (2) upfront policy budgets in the standing brief — time windows, distance, cost=$0, category vetoes; (3) preview-before-commit showing exactly what will be RSVP'd as whom (Duckbill); (4) digest + one-tap approve as the DEFAULT autonomy level, full-auto opt-in (Google Ask for Me; gentle default wins per Motion-vs-Reclaim); (5) receipts feed — every autonomous action logged with visible undo/un-RSVP within a defined window; (6) auto-demotion to confirm-mode on novelty or risk (new source, approval-gated group, any fee, first event of a new category).
3. **Missing requirement: CANCEL HYGIENE as a first-class feature.** Auto-un-RSVP on declines/conflicts, prompt organizer notification, per-user attendance-integrity score. Simultaneously the regulatory shield (bot no-show 4x is the legislated harm), the organizer-trust moat, and a genuine user feature ("never be the flake").
4. **Missing requirement: mistake-rate SLO + silent-failure detection.** Six in ten consumers churn after ONE visible agent mistake; the reconciliation loop (organizer cancel/reschedule detection) is the retention engine. Instrument wrong-RSVP and stale-calendar rates as top-line product metrics.
5. **Pricing:** anchor a paid tier at $15–30/mo (between Skej and Howie); free tier = discovery + ranking + digest only (discovery is free everywhere in-market). Reserve $50+/mo for a future "never miss / guaranteed spot" tier (Dorsia shows access, not recommendations, commands premium). Never introduce human ops staff into fulfillment — the only affordable human in a consumer concierge is the user's own tap.
6. **Positioning/wedge:** do not compete on "AI that RSVPs" — Google/OpenTable/Expedia will commoditize per-vertical acting. Position as the cross-source events layer with a standing brief and a reconciled calendar: "one brief, every source, your calendar stays true." Market the honest split ("does 95%, you tap once") as a trust feature, not a limitation — travel's market leader ships handoff-only, and the ~2% autonomous-booking stat says users don't want the last 5% automated yet.
7. **Frame delegation as a familiar human ritual, not AI-speak:** the NL standing brief is "briefing your concierge"; approvals arrive as a digest you tap. Consider an email/inbox surface for the digest — CC-the-assistant is the only delegation UX with a decade of consumer acceptance.
8. **ToS/optics requirement:** always act as the authenticated user with genuine intent; never speculative multi-booking to hold spots — exactly the behavior six state legislatures are outlawing in the adjacent restaurant space.
