# Indie Builds, Blog Writeups & Community Lessons (AI Event Finders/Digests)

> Two decades of indie event-discovery builds — HN launches, dev.to writeups, hobbyist RSVP bots, and one-person curated calendars — constitute the cheapest available field experiment on what dies (lists) and what survives (trust, push, action), and thus directly calibrate where the Events Concierge's defensible value must sit.

## Landscape inventory

### Canonical postmortems and category diagnosis

- **Hugecity + Hugh Malkin postmortem** (dead ~2015; postmortem essay canonical; http://www.hughmalkin.com/blogwriter/2015/9/23/why-no-one-has-solved-event-discovery) — Facebook-event aggregation + personalized discovery app; the founder's essay "Why no one has solved event discovery" dissects the whole category. Reached 1M monthly visitors and still failed on retention/monetization; free, and no model (ads, ticketing, deals) ever worked. The single best builder postmortem: 90% of users visit event apps ≤1x/week; demand is wave-shaped (50% of tickets at announce, 50% week-of); Facebook-RSVP is "aspirational"; discovery alone is unmonetizable. Crucially he names the CONCIERGE model (text-based personal booking via local tastemakers) as one of his viable escapes — direct validation of our framing.

### General aggregators / event search engines (the tarpit conveyor)

- **Radius** (active, verified live July 2026; https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40717398) — free Meetup.com alternative: groups, events, hyper-local activity feed. Show HN Jun 2024 hit 351 points/152 comments (biggest HN splash in the space); as of 2026 advertises 500+ communities with groups of 12-47 members — still tiny vs Meetup. Free, planned organizer freemium. Two years post-launch it remains sub-scale: proof that supply-side network effects, not software, are the moat. The thread documents Meetup organizer pain (~$30/mo) and the rule that "natural discoverability" drives 60%+ of attendance — ride existing supply (Meetup/Luma/Eventbrite) rather than rebuild it.
- **Wiwo** (stagnant/uncertain; Show HN Feb 2024, May 2024 repost got 1 point; https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39459456) — map-based aggregated event finder. 29 pts/36 comments; concept praised, but a 200k-resident region showed only 6-7 events. Canonical coverage-gap failure: users churn instantly on thin data; a commenter mislocated pins and another cited PG's "organic ideas" essay calling event aggregation a classic bad idea because sources constantly change — maintenance is the product.
- **VibrantSearch.ai** (active, launched Feb 2026; https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47122649) — semantic search over hundreds of thousands of crawled NYC event listings; map UI, date/location filters; experimenting with MCP connectors so LLM assistants can query it. 2 HN points; early. Closest new overlap with our DISCOVERY layer, in our launch metro; its MCP direction shows discovery is commoditizing into an API any assistant can call. Its thesis — surface small-venue events (pub trivia) that ticket-monetized platforms bury — is a coverage differentiator worth copying. It does NOT rank-to-taste, RSVP, or write calendars.
- **whereshouldwego.co** (active, May 2025; https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44029084) — single-search-box event search engine across 10 cities (NYC, SF, London, Berlin...) on Rails 8. 17 pts; positive comments ("found a cool board game event in London"). The recurring delight across these builds is "it found something I'd never have seen"; the recurring complaint is "add more sources". Depth-per-city beats city count.
- **gnistor.se / Ask HN "How do you find local events?"** (active; https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39622091) — Hugo static-site event aggregator; the Mar 2024 thread (14 pts/11 comments) also catalogs how HNers actually find events (Meetup, Slack groups, LinkedIn, word of mouth). Sharpest take: fragmented event discovery "can't be solved by a VC funded startup... only addressable by a non-profit". Our answer: the CONCIERGE ACTION, not aggregation, carries willingness to pay.
- **Mapibara / Murmurs / MatsuriMap / Gymmr** (active, near-zero traction each; https://mapibara.com) — 2024-2026 micro-launches: map-based local finders, friend-planning (Murmurs), Japan festivals, fitness events; 1-5 HN points each. A steady drumbeat of near-identical map/list finders launches and vanishes — the tarpit in action. None differentiate on ACTION (RSVP/calendar); all differentiate on presentation, which does not retain.
- **Citycal** (active, Show HN May 2026, 1 pt; https://citycal.com) — collaborative Wikipedia-style multi-city events calendar with user submissions. Crowdsourced supply historically fails on contributor cold-start; contrast with our crawl-first approach.
- **Wkndr** (active, Show HN Jan 2026, 2 pts; https://wkndr.app) — TikTok-style swipe feed for Melbourne weekend events, AI-generated visuals for events lacking media. Marks the 2026 UX experimentation frontier; also flags that long-tail events lack imagery — AI-generated event imagery is a polish idea.

### Vertical/niche entrants

- **Happenz.org** (active, Feb 2025; https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43010411) — London art-exhibition finder; 22 pts/10 comments. Vertical-niche builds get warmer receptions than general aggregators — supports interest-vertical onboarding rather than "all events".
- **OrangeWalrus** (active, Show HN Feb 2026, 3 pts; https://www.orangewalrus.com/) — aggregator for SF trivia nights and similar recurring small-venue events. Recurring venue events (trivia, open mics) are systematically missing from ticketed platforms — a coverage class our discovery should handle.

### DIY agent builds and commodity tooling

- **AI Event Butler** (active, personal use on n8n free tier; https://dev.to/varshithvhegde/i-built-an-ai-event-butler-so-id-never-miss-another-tech-meetup-and-you-can-too-37io) — n8n + Gemini + Bright Data scraping Eventbrite daily → curated tech-meetup email newsletter for Bangalore, with dedupe, Today/Tomorrow/This-Week buckets, jokey commentary. Single user: 3 months = 47 events he'd have missed, ~2h/week saved. Pain ranking: anti-bot walls were the hard part (solved by paying Bright Data), prompt engineering beat parsing logic, "personality" in the digest drove engagement. His roadmap gap = calendar integration — exactly our loop.
- **Tech Event Vista** (active, open source; https://dev.to/danishaft/how-i-built-a-tech-event-discovery-platform-with-real-time-scraping-3o4f) — Next.js/BullMQ/Redis platform scraping Luma + Eventbrite via Puppeteer with Apify fallback, daily Vercel cron, tech-events-only filter; no traction shared. Confirms per-source reality: Puppeteer blocked "almost immediately" by Eventbrite and Luma → stealth patches + paid scrapers; URL-based dedupe leaves cross-platform duplicates. Fuzzy entity-resolution dedupe is a missing requirement in most specs, arguably including ours.
- **n8n "AI-powered Local Event Finder" community template** (active, also mirrored by GrowwStacks; https://n8n.io/workflows/4816-ai-powered-local-event-finder-with-multi-tool-search/) — drop-in n8n agent: user criteria → Brave web/local search + Gemini → recommendations. Cloned across template marketplaces. DIY discovery is now copy-paste — a weekend n8n user can replicate our discovery+rank layer. Differentiation must live where templates can't go: credentialed RSVP execution, calendar reconciliation, multi-tenant trust.
- **GPT-wrapper "event finder agents"** (active, commodity; Taskade Networking Event Finder, Agent.AI networking-event-finder, agenthost.ai Groove, eventfinder.site, Apify phazonoverload/event-finder; https://apify.com/phazonoverload/event-finder) — thin LLM agents that take a natural-language ask and search/filter event datasets (e.g., developers.events) or the open web. Template-marketplace tier; no evidence of real usage. NL-in → event-list-out is fully commoditized. Our NL front-door is table stakes.
- **Teledigest** (active repo; https://github.com/igoropaniuk/teledigest) — LLM framework for Telegram digest bots: scrape channels → SQLite FTS → LLM summarize → publish digest. Telegram channels are both a SOURCE (many cities' events live in channels) and a cheap push DELIVERY surface; digest-bot plumbing is commodity OSS.

### Auto-RSVP and source-specific scrapers

- **Meetup auto-RSVP script cluster** (mostly archived, pre-2020, pre-LLM, several Selenium-based; jbeluch/meetup-rsvper, rschmukler/rsvp-me "RSVP sniper", alsugiharto/auto-meetup-rsvp-bot, akshansh2000, Bilsteen, tjlav5; https://github.com/rschmukler/rsvp-me) — half a dozen independent hobbyist scripts that auto-RSVP "yes" to Meetup events in the user's groups, some explicitly "snipers" for events that fill in minutes. Small stars each, but 6+ independent builds of the same thing: the strongest organic demand signal for our autonomous-RSVP wedge. "Guaranteed spot in fast-filling events" is a nameable, chargeable job-to-be-done, and groups-you-already-belong-to is precisely where they all operate (our permitted-RSVP tier).
- **djb-gt/resident-advisor-events-scraper** (active repo; https://github.com/djb-gt/resident-advisor-events-scraper) — Python scraper for Resident Advisor events (name, artists, venue, guest counts) per city/date range. Long-tail vertical sources have ready-made community scrapers; guest-count fields are a free popularity signal for ranking.

### The decades-long survivors: human curation

- **The Skint** (active since 2009; https://www.theskint.com/) — daily human-curated email of free/cheap NYC events (two friends' tip-list turned institution). 17 years running; subscriber count undisclosed; free to readers, advertiser/donation funded. The demand shape: a zero-tech, trusted-taste, push-medium product outlives every venture-backed discovery app in the same city. Retention = trust + email push, not search. Also a scrapable taste-encoded SOURCE for our NYC launch.
- **Funcheap SF** (active since 2003; https://sf.funcheap.com/about/) — curated SF Bay Area free/cheap events guide + daily email, ~3-person team; claims 150,000+ email subscribers after 23 years; free, ads + promoter listings. Ceiling estimate for single-metro curated events audience. Proves durable six-figure local demand for "tell me what to do" — and that it took two decades of human curation to build; an agent that matches this trust level faster is the opportunity.
- **19hz.info** (active ~14 years; https://19hz.info/) — anonymous one-person plain-HTML tables of electronic music events across ~16 US metros, genre/price tagged. Beloved precisely for information density, zero engagement tricks, and full price/genre metadata. Scene users prefer dense tables over swipe feeds — a UI lesson for our ranked results.
- **nyc-noise "sites like this" ecosystem + the-counterforce.org** (active, growing — new entries founded 2026; https://nyc-noise.com/sites-like-this/) — directory of ~40-50 one-person/small-team curated city event calendars worldwide (nonsense nyc since 2000, cringe.com Columbus since 1995, stressfaktor Berlin since 1998, mucnoise, bostonshows.org...), plus a "How to Start Your Own Show Calendar" playbook that spawned the multi-city "ask a punk" network. Decades of longevity across dozens of instances; several explicitly "big-tech-free" (cal.red, 2026). The long tail of supply our crawlers must ingest AND a cultural constraint: these communities are anti-algorithm and anti-scrape-and-resell; attribution/linking etiquette matters. Their persistence proves niche-taste curation is the retention engine general aggregators lack.

## Verified findings

1. **[adjusted] Event discovery is a formally recognized tarpit, and the 2024-2026 indie record confirms the mechanism.** Consumer event discovery is a named "tarpit idea" (YC/Dalton Caldwell — the original talk names apps to discover new events/restaurants/concerts/bands; the July 2024 "Tarpit Ideas: The Sequel" adds coordinating events with friends), and the 2024-2026 indie record confirms it: near-identical finder apps launch on HN, get warm feedback, then stall — Wiwo (29 pts, Feb 2024), Mapibara (5 pts, Dec 2025), Murmurs (3 pts, Nov 2025), Wkndr (2 pts, Jan 2026), Citycal (1 pt, May 2026). Even Radius (351 pts, June 2024, the category's biggest recent splash) is still alive but only advertises "500+ communities" two years later. A Wiwo commenter cited PG's essay calling the idea a typical "bad idea" to the builder's face, and an Ask HN commenter concluded the aggregation problem "can't be solved by a VC funded startup... only addressable by a non-profit." The trap is specifically DISCOVERY-AS-THE-PRODUCT; the record does not indict action/agent layers, which almost none of these attempts include.

2. **[adjusted] Data acquisition and coverage hygiene is the #1 technical killer.** Anti-bot walls at Eventbrite/Luma (Tech Event Vista's initial Puppeteer setup "got blocked almost immediately," pushing it onto stealth patches plus paid Apify scrapers), brittle/inconsistent HTML and data-quality issues that AI Event Butler only tamed by paying Bright Data ("solved 90% of my scraping headaches"), URL-only dedupe that leaves cross-platform duplicates, and long-tail coverage/geocoding gaps — Wiwo showed 6-7 events for the 200k-resident Burlington VT metro plus mislocated venue pins, drawing an immediate "not useful right now" verdict. Malkin's structural argument: comprehensive event databases can't be crowd-built because most events are shared privately inside walled gardens, leaving startups dependent on platform APIs they don't control. Validates our hybrid API-first + browser-fallback architecture as the real cost center, and surfaces missing spec requirements: fuzzy cross-source entity resolution (not URL dedupe), cancelled/stale-event hygiene, per-source freshness SLAs, a standing anti-bot budget.

3. **[adjusted] What survives for decades is trusted human niche curation.** The Skint (NYC email list, 2009-, still publishing July 2026), Funcheap SF (2003-, self-reported 150k+ email subs), Nonsense NYC (2000-), cringe.com (Columbus web calendar, 1995-, still updated weekly), 19hz.info (electronic-music web calendars for 16 metros, ~2012-), plus the ~40-50 DIY city calendars cataloged in the nyc-noise/Counterforce network (including 7 askapunk.* city sites; many but not all one-person operations). The email survivors (Skint, Funcheap, Nonsense) additionally show PUSH delivery sidesteps low-frequency retention — but 19hz and cringe.com prove long-lived pull websites survive too when trust and niche identity are strong. Trust/taste and niche identity are the universal survival properties; push delivery is a strong accelerant. These outlets are simultaneously our best long-tail SOURCES (they encode taste) and culturally anti-algorithm — ingest with attribution, don't strip-mine.

4. **[confirmed] Retention math for event products is structurally hostile.** Per Malkin's Hugecity postmortem: 90% of users looked for events once a week or less; ~50% of tickets sold at announce and ~50% week-of (Saturday peak, Monday trough); Facebook "attending" was aspirational while personal direct invitations drove real attendance; personalization collapses for socially disconnected users (cold-start). Fact-check caveats: Malkin lists FOUR (not five) escapes — niche focus, personal concierge (local tastemakers, travel-agency-style fee model), ticketing integration, and data insights — and the percentages are 2015 Hugecity-specific figures, directionally corroborated by modern industry U-curve ticketing data but not exact industry constants. Implication stands: our loop must convert intent into COMMITMENT (confirmed RSVP + calendar block + reminder), and the "I got you in" message should read like a personal invitation, not a listing.

5. **[unverifiable — medium confidence, absence-of-evidence] No one in the indie corpus closes the full loop.** Across ~20 inventoried builds, zero perform the RSVP or write-and-reconcile the calendar; AI Event Butler lists "calendar integration" as unbuilt roadmap. Meanwhile 6+ unrelated hobbyists each hand-rolled Meetup auto-RSVP bots because desirable free events fill within minutes — precisely in the "groups you already belong to" tier where our spec permits autonomous RSVP. "Never miss a spot in fast-filling free events" is a concrete, chargeable job-to-be-done; RSVP-sniping deserves first-class treatment (watch for open spots, instant RSVP, waitlist-promotion detection). Caveat: this is absence-of-evidence across the indie corpus only; funded competitors are covered in other dossiers.

6. **[high confidence, non-load-bearing] The discovery+rank layer is a weekend template.** n8n marketplace workflows, GPT-store agents, Apify actors, and new semantic-search entrants (VibrantSearch NYC, Feb 2026, already MCP-exposed to LLM assistants) have collapsed NL-in → ranked-events-out to commodity. Consequences: (1) defensibility cannot be the NL interface or the ranked list — it must be credentialed action, calendar reconciliation, coverage depth, multi-tenant trust; (2) commodity indexes like VibrantSearch could be ingested as sources rather than rebuilt.

7. **[high confidence, non-load-bearing] Delight moments are consistent and specific.** (1) "It found something great I'd never have seen" (long-tail/small-venue events — Wiwo's warmest comment, whereshouldwego's board-game find); (2) quantified time saved (~2h/week, 47 missed events in 3 months for AI Event Butler); (3) personality/voice in the digest (kept the Butler's author actually reading it); (4) winning a spot in a fast-filling event. Delight is long-tail discovery + effort removal, not comprehensiveness for its own sake.

8. **[medium confidence, non-load-bearing] Meetup organizer diaspora is real but slow.** Meetup's organizer-hostile pricing (~$30/mo basic) keeps generating alternatives (Radius, gel, cal.red "big-tech-free"), yet Meetup's discoverability locks most groups in ("60%+ of attendance from natural discoverability"). Meetup remains the highest-value permitted-RSVP source at launch, but connector abstraction should assume organizer migration toward Luma/indie calendars over 2026-27.

## Product implications

1. **Position as the ACTION layer, never the discovery app.** The deliverable is a confirmed spot on your calendar ("I got you in Friday 7pm, it's on your calendar"), not a list. Lists are a YC-certified tarpit and n8n-template commodities; every dead indie build stopped at the list.
2. **Design retention around PUSH + the calendar, not app opens.** Event usage is structurally low-frequency (90% weekly-or-less, wave-shaped demand). Ship a weekly taste-tuned digest (email/Telegram/WhatsApp) whose every item carries a one-tap handoff; treat the user's calendar as the persistent UI surface. The 20-year survivors won on trusted voice (+push where email-based) — give the concierge a voice/personality; it measurably drives digest engagement.
3. **Make "never miss a fast-filling event" a first-class, nameable feature (RSVP sniping).** Watch target groups/venues, RSVP within seconds of open, detect waitlist promotions. Six independent hobbyist Meetup-sniper repos prove organic demand, and it lives exactly in our permitted-autonomy tier. This is the single clearest chargeable moment in the free-RSVP scope.
4. **Launch ONE metro deep (NYC) with an explicit coverage bar before any expansion.** Thin coverage killed Wiwo-class builds instantly. Coverage must include the non-ticketed long tail (trivia nights, gallery openings, DIY shows) via the curated-human layer — ingest The Skint, nonsense nyc, 19hz-class calendars and ICS feeds WITH attribution/linkbacks (these communities are anti-scrape-and-resell; etiquette is a distribution asset, not just ethics). Funcheap's self-reported 150k subs in SF is the realistic single-metro demand ceiling to plan against.
5. **Price the concierge action, not discovery.** Consumers demonstrably won't pay for event lists (17-23-year-old curated newsletters still can't charge readers; ads/donations only). Free tier = digest + handoff links; paid tier = autonomous execution (auto-RSVP, sniping, calendar reconciliation, cancel/reschedule guard). First willingness-to-pay hypothesis to test: "guaranteed spots + zero effort", not "better recommendations".
6. **Add missing spec requirements surfaced by builder pain:** (a) fuzzy cross-source event entity resolution — URL dedupe demonstrably insufficient; (b) cancelled/stale/moved-event hygiene with per-source freshness SLAs; (c) a standing anti-bot line item (residential proxy / Bright Data-class spend) for Eventbrite+Luma — every indie who scraped them got blocked immediately; (d) taste cold-start onboarding (explicit interest vertical + a few example events), since personalization provably fails for socially disconnected users; (e) AI-generated imagery fallback for long-tail events lacking media.
7. **Frame confirmations as personal invitations.** The Hugecity postmortem's core behavioral finding: personal invites and commitment drive attendance while browsing does not. Post-RSVP messaging, reminders, and a lightweight "bring a friend" forward loop should mimic invitation dynamics; the concierge should ask for post-event feedback to close the taste loop.
8. **Treat commodity discovery indexes (VibrantSearch-class, MCP-exposed) as potential SOURCES/partners rather than competitors**, and keep the Meetup connector abstracted — organizer diaspora toward Luma and indie calendars is real and ongoing.
