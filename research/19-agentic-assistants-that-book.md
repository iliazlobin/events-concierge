# Dossier 19 — General-Purpose Agentic Assistants That Can Book/RSVP on the Web

> Whether horizontal agents (ChatGPT agent, Gemini, Claude, Comet, Manus, …) can already do our job determines if Events Concierge is a product or a feature — this dossier maps the substitute threat and the pattern library it leaves behind (as of July 2026).

## Landscape inventory

### Frontier-lab agent modes and their casualties

- **OpenAI Operator** (dead, Aug 31 2025; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_Operator) — First consumer computer-using agent: drove a cloud browser to book restaurants, buy tickets, order groceries; launched Jan 23 2025 as a $200/mo Pro-only research preview with OpenTable, StubHub, Priceline, Instacart partners. Traction: research-preview scale only; deprecated ~7 months after launch. Relevance: the founding postmortem of the category — retired amid reliability gaps on commerce flows (CAPTCHAs, complex JS checkouts, session management) and folded into ChatGPT agent. Confirms browser automation as best-effort fallback, not spine — matching our hybrid API-first architecture.
- **ChatGPT agent mode** (active; https://chatgpt.com/features/agent/) — Operator's successor inside ChatGPT: visual browser, text browser, terminal, API access; research, form-filling, spreadsheets; can book restaurants and appointments. Pauses entirely on logins and payments and hands the browser back to the user; consequential actions require confirmation ("supervised project runner"); tasks take 5–30 minutes. Pricing: Plus $20/mo (~40 agent messages/mo), Pro $100–200/mo (~400/mo); no free tier. Traction: largest distribution of any agent, but rationed; independent testing flags calendar automation (time zones, conflicts, booking judgment) as one of its most consistent failure points. Relevance: the main substitute threat — and it structurally cannot do our core loop: it stops at exactly the login/RSVP/payment step we automate, runs no recurring discovery, and is bad at calendar work; ~40 msgs/mo makes continuous event monitoring impossible.
- **ChatGPT Atlas browser** (dead — announced Jul 9 2026, sunset Aug 9 2026; https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/openai-is-shutting-down-atlas-but-its-ai-browser-ambitions-are-still-growing/) — OpenAI's standalone agentic browser (Oct 2025) with agent mode for travel/booking; killed after 9 months, agentic browsing redistributed into the ChatGPT desktop app and a Chrome extension. Failed to displace Chrome; part of Fidji Simo's "cut side quests" purge. Relevance: fresh (this week) evidence that even OpenAI concluded the browser is a feature, not the destination — horizontal agentic browsers are consolidating, leaving vertical task-completion open.
- **Google Project Mariner → Gemini Agent + Chrome Auto Browse** (Mariner dead May 4 2026, tech folded into Gemini; https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/05/google-shuts-down-project-mariner-ai-agent.html) — Mariner (Dec 2024) ran up to 10 parallel visual browser tasks; killed after 17 months. Successors: Gemini Agent in-app, AI Mode ticket-finding in Search (fan-out across ticket sites, fills forms), Chrome Auto Browse (US, AI Pro/Ultra). Gemini asks confirmation before comms, purchases, and web-form submissions; usage caps even at Ultra ($100–200/mo tiers). Identified by a "Google-Agent" user agent since Mar 2026; Auto Browse expanding to Android OS-level on ~200M devices (Jun 2026). Relevance: the horizontal player most likely to commoditize one-shot PAID ticket buying inside Search; its confirmation-gated, capped model still leaves recurring free-community-event RSVP + calendar reconciliation untouched.
- **Claude for Chrome (Anthropic)** (active beta, all paid plans since Dec 2025; https://claude.com/claude-for-chrome) — Chrome extension letting Claude navigate, click, and fill forms in the user's real browser. Per-site pre-approval; asks before irreversible actions like purchases; docs explicitly advise against financial transactions, password management, and sensitive data; flags prompt injection as a non-foolproof risk. Bundled with Claude Pro $20/mo+. Relevance: the most safety-conservative autonomy UX in the category — even the best-in-class consumer browser agent won't autonomously transact. A vertical concierge with a narrow allowlisted action set (RSVP-only, no payments) can credibly offer MORE autonomy than horizontal agents dare.
- **Microsoft Edge Copilot Mode** (active; Advanced Actions in preview; https://nohacks.co/blog/agentic-browser-landscape-2026) — Free agentic mode in Edge: multi-tab awareness, voice navigation; "Advanced Actions" for reservations still in preview. Relevance: even free OS-bundled agents keep booking actions gated in preview — autonomous transactional reliability remains the industry's open problem.

### Commerce protocols and platform-mediated checkout

- **ChatGPT Apps + Agentic Commerce Protocol (Ticketmaster/StubHub/SeatGeek)** (active; Instant Checkout leg dead Mar 2026; https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/04/10/ticketmaster-integrates-chatgpt/) — MCP-based apps ecosystem: StubHub app (Dec 2025, 10M+ live events), Ticketmaster app (Apr 9 2026, US/CA, all tiers incl. free), SeatGeek (Mar 2026) — conversational ticket DISCOVERY with handoff to the platform for checkout. In-chat Instant Checkout (Stripe ACP, 4% merchant fee) was discontinued Mar 2026 after only ~30 Shopify merchants went live and Walmart's in-chat conversion ran ~1/3 of its own site; OpenAI pivoted discovery-first with merchants owning checkout. Relevance: (1) paid ticketed event discovery is already aggregated by ChatGPT — do not compete there; (2) the industry's own experiment concluded agent-does-discovery + merchant-owns-checkout converts better — direct validation of our honest-split one-tap-handoff framing. Free community events (Meetup, Luma, Partiful) have NO apps here.
- **Amazon Buy For Me / Rufus → Alexa for Shopping** (active; Rufus renamed May 13 2026; https://stellagent.ai/insights/amazon-rufus-buy-for-me) — Agentic checkout: Amazon's agent visits external brand sites, adds to cart, checks out with encrypted user payment data (Claude Sonnet + Nova via Bedrock); also scheduled autonomous re-purchase on price drops. Free to Amazon customers; pilot Apr 2025 → 100M+ products / 400k+ merchants by Mar 2026 (some Shopify merchants included without opt-in). Relevance: proof that unattended agentic checkout works at scale when ONE side controls payment rails and identity — platform-mediated, not adversarial browser automation. Not an events threat, but the pattern for why platform-permitted RSVP (e.g. Meetup member groups) is our reliable tier. Also the irony fueling Perplexity's defense.

### Independent agent startups (revenue proof and cautionary tales)

- **Perplexity Comet** (active; https://www.perplexity.ai/comet) — Agentic browser with in-page assistant: books restaurants/flights, manages email, fills forms, multi-step tasks. Free since Oct 2025 (was $200/mo Max); Android Nov 2025, iOS Mar 2026; Claude Opus 4.6 default agent model for Max subs since Apr 2026. Largest free agentic browser — and the category's cautionary tale: Brave found indirect prompt-injection vulns (invisible text, screenshot-embedded instructions); one review had it email the wrong contact when booking a table; a security analysis found it ~85% more vulnerable to phishing than Chrome. Relevance: closest behavioral substitute for one-off "book me something" tasks, and source of the two biggest pattern-library lessons — prompt injection on untrusted web content, and the Amazon injunction below.
- **Manus** (active; https://sacra.com/c/manus/) — General autonomous agent in a sandboxed cloud VM (real browser, terminal, filesystem); excels at research→deliverable tasks. Browser Operator extension (Nov 2025) controls the user's LOCAL browser with their credentials, sidestepping login walls/CAPTCHAs via trusted IP. Credit-based, ~$19–199/mo across 4 tiers. Traction: ~$450M annualized revenue Jun 2026 (Sacra; from $127M in 2025); 500k waitlist at Mar 2025 launch. Relevance: proves consumers/prosumers pay real money for delegation; its local-browser-with-user-credentials pattern is our best-effort RSVP leg's least-legally-risky variant (user's own device, IP, session).
- **Genspark Super Agent + Genspark Browser** (active; https://sacra.com/c/genspark/) — Agentic workspace turning NL requests into deliverables; AI browser (Jun 2025) with Autopilot Mode and 700+ tools; can make phone calls to book reservations (Call For Me). Free 100 credits/day; Plus $24.99/mo; Pro $249.99/mo; Team $30/seat. ~$250M annualized revenue Mar 2026; $200M+ Series B Nov 2025 at $1B+ valuation. Relevance: second revenue proof point plus the phone-call-booking pattern (voice as universal fallback where no API/RSVP button exists — venues/restaurants more than free events).
- **Lindy** (active; https://www.lindy.ai/pricing) — No-code personal-AI-agent builder; flagship use cases: meeting scheduling, inbox management, follow-ups; computer use on Pro tier; works over iMessage/SMS. Plus $49.99/mo, Pro $99.99/mo, Max $199.99/mo; free plan removed; unpublished usage quotas that silently pause the agent. Established prosumer/SMB niche; scheduling cited as its "cleanest use case." Relevance: nearest live product to "agent that manages your calendar" — but it coordinates MEETINGS with humans, not event discovery/RSVP. Opaque credit pricing is a UX anti-pattern to avoid; reliability-via-narrow-scope is the pattern to copy.
- **MultiOn → Please AI → AGI-0 (AGI Inc.)** (pivoted twice: →Please Jan 2025, →AGI-0 mobile app 2026; https://multion.ai/) — Once the highest-profile consumer web-agent startup (Div Garg, Stanford, 2022; early demos literally booked flights and RSVPed to events). Now a mobile agent app: rides, price comparisons; free tier 50 "action points"/mo. Traction weak/opaque — no new funding as of May 2026; serial rebrands signal no consumer PMF. Relevance: the cautionary tale for a HORIZONTAL consumer "agent that does everything": first mover, strong demos, no retention. Consumers pay for a finished vertical job, not general web-driving.
- **Opera Neon** (active; https://nohacks.co/blog/agentic-browser-landscape-2026) — Paid agentic browser with four specialized agents (Do, Make, ODRA, Chat); multi-model incl. Gemini 3 Pro. $19.90/mo; niche. Relevance: data point that some consumers pay ~$20/mo for agentic browsing alone — pricing anchor.
- **Dia (The Browser Company)** (acquired by Atlassian Sept 2025, ~$610M reported; enterprise pivot; https://nohacks.co/blog/agentic-browser-landscape-2026) — AI-native browser for knowledge workers; consumer momentum ceded. Relevance: another consumer agentic-browser exit-by-acquisition rather than consumer PMF.
- **Fellou** (active; https://nohacks.co/blog/agentic-browser-landscape-2026) — Agentic browser emphasizing workflow inspection/editing before execution; handles authenticated sessions (Salesforce, LinkedIn, Reddit). Pricing unclear; niche. Relevance: its "show the plan, let the user edit, then execute" UX is a good pattern for our RSVP confirmation-policy surface.

### Infrastructure, rules of the road, and evidence

- **Browser Use (OSS + Cloud)** (active; https://github.com/browser-use/browser-use) — Dominant open-source browser-agent library (Python/TS, MIT, any LLM); Cloud runs hosted sessions with residential proxies in 195 countries, per-minute billing. OSS free; Cloud from $29/mo. 95k+ GitHub stars; SOC 2 Type 2 (Oct 2025). Relevance: infrastructure, not competitor — likely build-vs-buy component for our best-effort RSVP leg (Stagehand v3, Skyvern, Browserbase as alternates). Its residential-proxy positioning is exactly what the Amazon ruling makes legally radioactive for authenticated actions.
- **Amazon v. Perplexity (Comet shopping agent lawsuit)** (active litigation; preliminary injunction Mar 10 2026, stayed by Ninth Circuit ~a week later; https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/10/amazon-wins-court-order-to-block-perplexitys-ai-shopping-agent.html) — Amazon sued (Nov 2025) over Comet logging into user Amazon accounts and buying while spoofing Chrome headers. Judge Chesney found Amazon likely to succeed on CFAA + California computer-fraud claims — user permission does not equal site authorization in password-protected systems. Ninth Circuit heard argument Jun 11 2026 (decision pending); ACLU/Knight Institute amici. Relevance: the legal landmine under our browser-automation RSVP leg — disguised agent traffic against objecting sources is injunction territory. Our spec needs an explicit agent-identity / no-spoofing / honor-blocks policy.
- **Cloudflare Signed Agents / Web Bot Auth (merchant side)** (active; production Mar 2026; default blocks Sept 15 2026; https://blog.cloudflare.com/signed-agents/) — IETF-track cryptographic agent identification (RFC 9421 HTTP Message Signatures). From Sept 15 2026 the "Agent" traffic category is blocked by default on ad-carrying pages; sites can allow only Web-Bot-Auth-verified agents. Recognized signers: Anthropic, OpenAI, Perplexity, Google. Context: bots are now 57.5% of HTML web traffic; ticketing-industry pieces project 37% of UK ticket sales agent-mediated by 2028 and push "let trusted agents in, block scalpers." Relevance: the gate our browser leg must pass — stealth automation gets structurally harder, but a registered, signed, well-behaved vertical agent could be whitelisted where anonymous horizontal agents are blocked. A real moat if we pursue signed-agent status.
- **NN/g ChatGPT Agent usability study** (2025; https://www.nngroup.com/articles/impressions-chatgpt-agent/) — Usability testing on real tasks incl. restaurant booking: verdict "Successful, Shaky, and Slow" — 55 seconds just to open a restaurant site; a booking succeeded but was error-prone; agent stalled when booking required WhatsApp and handed back to the human. Relevance: the best independent evidence of what booking via a horizontal agent feels like — works sometimes, slowly, under supervision. A vertical concierge's bar is "seconds, unattended, reconciled," which no horizontal agent meets.
- **Termo "Luma Event Manager" AI skill** (active, minor; https://termo.ai/skills/luma-event-manager) — Packaged agent skill for managing Luma events; marketplace-of-skills approach. Negligible traction. Relevance: early signal that event-platform automation is emerging as a skill/plugin layer on general agents; confirms Luma's surface is automatable. Luma itself reports ~2M people/month registering for events — the free-event pool is large.

## Verified findings

1. **[confirmed] Every major horizontal agent gates or refuses exactly the step our product automates: unattended login, RSVP/registration submission, and payment.** ChatGPT agent mode "pauses entirely" on logins and payments and hands the browser back; Claude for Chrome requires per-site pre-approval, asks before purchases, and officially advises against financial transactions and password use; Gemini asks confirmation before purchases AND before submitting web forms; Edge's reservation "Advanced Actions" remain preview-only (US, Win11/macOS); even Comet — the most autonomous — pauses and asks permission before logins and purchases per Perplexity's own help center. A form submission (which is what an RSVP is) is confirmation-gated across the board. Nuance from verification: these are mostly confirmation gates (some per-site waivable) rather than hard refusals, but no horizontal agent ships unattended login + RSVP submission + payment as of July 2026. "Autonomous RSVP where the source permits + reconciled calendar" is genuine white space, not an unmarketed feature.

2. **[adjusted] The industry converged on "agent does discovery, merchant completes the transaction" after in-chat checkout failed — independently validating the honest-split + one-tap-handoff framing.** OpenAI discontinued Instant Checkout in March 2026 (~6 months after its Sept 2025 launch): only ~30 Shopify merchants ever went live (vs "over a million coming soon" promised — the earlier "<15" figure did not survive fact-check), Walmart's in-chat conversion was ~1/3 of its own site's, and scraped product data was chronically stale; OpenAI pivoted to discovery-first merchant apps with checkout handled by the retailer. Operator was deprecated Aug 2025 in favor of ChatGPT agent after persistent reliability limits (38% OSWorld, 58% WebArena) — full-autonomy web-transaction agents did not survive as standalone products (note: attribution of the shutdown specifically to "unreliable purchases" was an overstatement; it was product consolidation atop weak reliability). Ticketmaster (Apr 9 2026), StubHub (Dec 18 2025), and SeatGeek (Mar 31 2026) all integrated into ChatGPT as DISCOVERY apps with an explicit handoff to their own checkout — Ticketmaster's announcement literally calls it "a streamlined handoff into the secure Ticketmaster marketplace." Our pre-filled one-tap human handoff is the pattern the market's biggest players landed on after burning money on full autonomy.

3. **[adjusted] Amazon v. Perplexity (preliminary injunction, Mar 10 2026, N.D. Cal.) is the leading authority that user permission does NOT equal site authorization** — Judge Chesney found Amazon likely to succeed on federal CFAA and California §502 claims against Comet, which spoofed Chrome user-agent headers and circumvented an Amazon technical block within 24 hours. Two corrections from verification: the ruling was Mar 10 (not Mar 9), and the Ninth Circuit STAYED the injunction about a week later (Comet has continued operating on Amazon during appeal; oral argument Jun 11 2026, decision pending) — so this is a stayed district-court preliminary finding, not settled law. Separately confirmed: Cloudflare's Web Bot Auth went production Mar 2026, and from Sept 15 2026 "Agent" (and "Training") traffic categories are blocked by default on ad-carrying pages. The strategic consequences hold regardless of appeal outcome: run browser-automation RSVP as the user's own session on their own device/IP, never spoof identity, honor blocks, and register as a signed agent — stealth automation is now both legally and technically a dead end against unwilling sources.

4. **[adjusted] Horizontal agents' reliability reputation on booking-class tasks remains poor — slow, supervised, and error-prone — and their economics (usage caps) prohibit continuous event monitoring.** Verified anchors: NN/g's "Successful, Shaky, and Slow" (55s to load a restaurant site, an 11-minute booking a human does in ~2 minutes, completed with unclarified assumptions); IBM's Comet review documents a table-booking prompt that emailed the wrong contact (correct attribution — the Medium post originally cited did not contain it); independent testing flags ChatGPT Agent's calendar automation as one of its most consistent failure points; ChatGPT Plus allots ~40 agent messages/month (Pro ~400) with scheduled-task runs counting against the cap — too few for daily discovery+RSVP+reconciliation loops. Two details did not survive fact-check and are dropped: the "19 minutes for a 3-minute task" / WhatsApp-stall anecdotes were unsourced as written, and the "~30 points below coding agents" benchmark gap is stale — OSWorld SOTA is ~72–85% by mid-2026, at/above the human baseline. Consequence: our durable differentiation is latency, unsupervised determinism, and always-on economics — NOT a benchmark-accuracy gap, which is closing.

5. **[unverifiable — researcher confidence: medium] The horizontal field consolidated violently in 2025–26 — standalone agent products died into chat-assistant features — while PAID-event discovery got absorbed into ChatGPT; the surviving white space is free/community events end-to-end.** Dead or pivoted within ~18 months: Operator (Aug 2025), Atlas (Jul 2026), Project Mariner (May 2026), MultiOn→Please→AGI-0, Dia (acquired). What survived: agent modes inside ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude with confirmation gates, plus discovery apps for Ticketmaster/StubHub/SeatGeek. Meetup, Luma, Partiful, and free Eventbrite events have NO ChatGPT app and no platform agent integration found — while Luma alone sees ~2M monthly registrations. Platforms aggregate high-GMV paid ticketing first because they monetize via commerce fees; free community RSVP + taste-ranking + calendar reconciliation has no fee pool to attract them. (Not fact-checked as a bundle; the individual death dates match the confirmed inventory above.)

6. **[researcher confidence: high] Prompt injection on untrusted web content is a demonstrated, unsolved, systemic vulnerability of browser agents.** Brave demonstrated indirect prompt injection against Comet (white-on-white text, HTML comments, instructions hidden in screenshots) causing cross-site actions like fetching email OTPs; Anthropic openly says its safeguards "aren't foolproof"; March 2026 "PleaseFix" vulnerabilities hit agents in authenticated sessions. Event pages are exactly the untrusted content an events agent ingests. A vertical agent whose action space is a hard-coded allowlist (RSVP endpoints, calendar writes — never email, payments, or arbitrary navigation) can make a categorical safety claim horizontal agents cannot.

7. **[researcher confidence: medium] Consumers demonstrably pay $19–$250/mo for agentic delegation at scale.** Manus ~$450M annualized revenue (Jun 2026, Sacra), $19–199/mo tiers; Genspark ~$250M annualized (Mar 2026), $1B+ valuation; Lindy $49.99–199.99/mo; Opera Neon $19.90/mo; ChatGPT Plus $20 with agent rationing. Actual delegated usage skews to research/data/forms; booking tasks are demo-famous but usage-light. Nobody reports a recurring "watch for events I'd like, sign me up, keep my calendar true" workflow on any horizontal agent.

8. **[researcher confidence: medium] Where a horizontal agent DOES beat us: one-off, novel, cross-domain tasks and paid-ticket discovery with big-brand inventory.** ChatGPT users can already ask Ticketmaster/StubHub apps for Saturday's game tickets; Gemini AI Mode fan-outs across ticket sites with live pricing; Manus/Genspark handle arbitrary "plan my weekend" one-shots. What none of them own: persistent taste models, standing NL subscriptions, source-permitted auto-RSVP, and post-RSVP reconciliation (organizer cancels/reschedules, un-RSVP). Our defensibility is the recurring loop, not the single transaction.

9. **[researcher confidence: medium] Manus's Browser Operator pattern (agent drives the user's LOCAL browser with their own credentials and IP) is the legally and technically safest architecture for our best-effort RSVP leg.** Shipped Nov 2025 explicitly to avoid login prompts and CAPTCHAs via trusted IP; it sidesteps the Amazon/Perplexity fact pattern (datacenter traffic spoofing human headers) because traffic genuinely originates from the user's device and session. Trade-off: requires an installed extension/desktop presence, complicating a pure-cloud multi-tenant design — a first-class architecture decision for the Luma/Partiful best-effort tier.

10. **[researcher confidence: medium] Ticketing merchants are moving from "block all bots" to "admit verified trusted agents, block scalpers."** Industry analyses (Edgar Dunn, Security Boulevard, DataDome) frame agentic commerce as revenue (projected 37% of UK ticket sales by 2028); Cloudflare's signed-agents registry is the mechanism (Anthropic, OpenAI, Perplexity, Google already verified). No explicit public Eventbrite/Meetup agent policy found; the new fact is a whitelisting rail that did not exist in 2025.

## Product implications

1. **Position against the horizontal agents' gap, not against event platforms.** The pitch contrast: ChatGPT agent = 5–30 min, supervised, ~40 runs/month, pauses at every login; Events Concierge = continuous background discovery, source-permitted auto-RSVP in seconds, calendar always reconciled. "General agents stop and ask you at the RSVP; we finish the job."
2. **Keep and market the honest split as the industry-validated pattern, not a limitation.** Instant Checkout's death (Mar 2026) and the Ticketmaster/StubHub/SeatGeek discovery-app model prove agent-discovers + human-taps-checkout converts better. Never re-litigate in-flow paid checkout at launch.
3. **MISSING REQUIREMENT — agent-identity and traffic policy.** No user-agent spoofing; honor robots/blocks; prefer user-session/user-IP execution for best-effort RSVP (Manus Browser Operator pattern); pursue Cloudflare Web Bot Auth signed-agent registration. Amazon v. Perplexity makes disguised automation against unwilling sources injunction-grade risk (even with the injunction stayed pending appeal), and Cloudflare default-blocks anonymous agent traffic from Sept 15 2026.
4. **MISSING REQUIREMENT — prompt-injection hardening for ingested event pages.** Treat all scraped event content as untrusted; the action space must be a hard allowlist (RSVP endpoints + calendar writes only — never email, payment, or arbitrary navigation). Turn this into a marketable safety claim horizontal agents cannot make.
5. **Make the confirmation-policy matrix a first-class product surface** (per-source, per-action: auto / notify / confirm), borrowing Fellou's inspect-plan-then-run UX and Claude-in-Chrome's per-site pre-approval — the trust onboarding ramp from handoff-everything to autonomous-where-permitted.
6. **Wedge and ICP: urban free/community event goers** (Meetup, Luma, Partiful, free Eventbrite) — the segment ChatGPT's ticketing apps and Gemini's AI-Mode ticket buying do NOT serve because there is no commerce fee pool. Do not compete on paid mainstream tickets; integrate or deep-link those instead.
7. **Pricing:** free tier = discovery + one-tap handoff (habit builder); paid ~$10–15/mo = auto-RSVP + reconciliation + standing subscriptions — deliberately under the $20 ChatGPT Plus anchor (we are a companion, not a replacement); the Manus/Genspark $19–25 anchor caps headroom. Avoid Lindy-style opaque credits — flat monthly with clear limits.
8. **Speed/latency SLO as an explicit differentiator and marketing number:** connector-based RSVP in under 60 seconds unattended vs 5–30-minute supervised runs (NN/g's 11-minute booking for a ~2-minute human task is the quotable enemy benchmark).
9. **Quarterly wedge-erosion watch-list:** (a) a Meetup/Luma/Partiful app appearing in ChatGPT's app directory; (b) Gemini Auto Browse relaxing its form-submission confirmation gate; (c) ChatGPT agent raising usage caps or adding recurring scheduled tasks; (d) the Ninth Circuit ruling in Amazon v. Perplexity (a reversal would re-open aggressive automation for everyone — cutting both ways). Note the reliability window is finite: benchmark accuracy is closing fast (OSWorld at/above human baseline), so build the moat on economics and statefulness, not agent incompetence.
10. **The recurring reconciliation loop is the deepest moat** — organizer cancel/reschedule detection, un-RSVP, calendar truth. It is stateful, boring, per-source, fee-less work structurally unattractive to every platform player surveyed. Invest in connector depth there rather than breadth of one-shot booking.
