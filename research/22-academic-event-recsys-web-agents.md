# Academic Prior Art: Event Recommendation & Web-Agent Booking Tasks

> The academic record sets both our ranking-accuracy ceiling and our autonomous-action reliability floor — the two numbers that determine whether "autonomous concierge" is an honest product promise or a liability.

## Landscape inventory

### Classical EBSN event recommendation (the signal canon)

- **Context-Aware Event Recommendation in Event-based Social Networks — Macedo, Marinho, Santos** (paper, RecSys 2015; https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2792838.2800187) — The canonical EBSN event-rec paper. Learning-to-rank over five contextual signal families — content (event description), collaborative (RSVPs), social (group memberships), location (geo preference), temporal (time-of-week preference) — on large Meetup crawls (Phoenix, Chicago, San Jose). RecSys 2015 distinguished-paper recognition; most-cited event-rec paper of its generation, with up to 79% NDCG@10 improvement over non-contextual baselines. Relevance: directly defines OUR ranking feature set (geo distance, group membership, time-of-week, event-text content) and frames event rec as intrinsically item-cold-start — our ranker should be content+context-first by design, not as a fallback.
- **Event Recommendation in Event-based Social Networks — Macedo & Marinho** (paper, 2014 doctoral symposium; https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1210/SP2014_02.pdf) — Precursor laying out the Meetup RSVP-prediction problem, the short-lifetime/cold-start framing, and the multi-signal hybrid approach; combining signals notably beats any single signal, and geo distance alone is weaker than social+temporal. Foundational, heavily cited. Relevance: hybrid signal fusion is table stakes; a warning against "nearest events first" as the default sort.
- **A Survey of Context-Aware Recommendation Schemes in Event-Based Social Networks** (paper, Electronics/MDPI 2020; https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/9/10/1583) — Survey of the whole EBSN rec literature (Meetup/Plancast/DoubanEvent): context taxonomies, methods, reported accuracy ranges. Standard survey reference. Relevance: sets the realistic accuracy ceiling — event-rec ranking quality is far below movie/e-commerce recsys because every item is one-shot. We must not promise "it learns exactly what you like"; we promise a good explainable shortlist.
- **Collaborative Social Group Influence for Event Recommendation** (paper, CIKM 2016; https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2983323.2983879) — Models group-membership influence on event attendance; group/social context materially lifts RSVP prediction. Well-cited. Relevance: empirical backing for our wedge — the user's EXISTING Meetup group memberships are among the strongest attendance predictors, the same surface where our autonomous RSVP is permitted. Ranking signal and actuation permission coincide.
- **Latent Networks Fusion for event recommendation in ephemeral (offline) social networks** (paper; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.1118) — Early work explicitly modeling the EPHEMERAL nature of events/attendee networks that dissolve after the event. Moderately cited. Relevance: names the structural property our ranking layer must respect — no per-item rating accumulation, ever. Signals must transfer across events (venue, organizer, topic, time slot); organizer- and venue-level priors are the durable entities.
- **Heterogeneous Social Network + Time Information event recommendation** (paper, ACM 2023; https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3573834.3574514) — Event rec combining heterogeneous EBSN relations with explicit time information; notes implicit-feedback-only data, sparsity, and 10M+ MAU scale of Meetup/Eventbrite/Douban. Minor venue, representative of the late classical wave. Relevance: feedback is implicit (attendance, not ratings) — our learning loop should treat RSVP-kept vs RSVP-bailed vs handoff-declined as the signal hierarchy, with time-of-week preference as an explicit learned feature.
- **EventRec: Personalized Event Recommendations for Smart EBSNs** (paper, 2017; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317639303_EventRec_Personalized_Event_Recommendations_for_Smart_Event-Based_Social_Networks) — Contextual-learning recommender on Meetup crawls; claims precision/recall gains for both regular AND cold-start users. Moderately cited. Relevance: user-side cold start is solvable with context + explicit preference elicitation — supports a short onboarding taste-interview rather than waiting to learn from behavior.

### Group event recommendation (the "plan with friends" subfield)

- **GMAN: Group event recommendation via graph multi-head attention** (paper, Information Processing & Management 2022; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306457321002752) — Graph multi-head attention over explicit+implicit EBSN relations to recommend events to GROUPS of users, addressing sparsity and cold start. Representative of the 2020-2022 deep-learning wave (with SRGAM, ACGER). Relevance: group event rec is a mature subfield — "plan something for me and my friends" is a literature-supported future feature, structurally different from single-user ranking (preference aggregation, influence weights).
- **SRGAM: Social Relationship-based Graph Attention Model for group event recommendation** (paper, 2020; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2020/8834450) — Graph attention over social relations on Meetup/Plancast/Douban-style data; tackles sparsity/cold start via social edges. Cited within the niche. Relevance: social graph edges substitute for missing item history — if we ever ingest a user's social/attendee graph, it is a cold-start remedy, not just a growth feature.

### LLM-era techniques that transfer to events

- **CoS: Towards Optimal Event Scheduling via Chain-of-Scheduling** (paper, arXiv 2511.12913, 2025; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.12913) — LLM chain-of-scheduling for recommending SCHEDULES of events (bundles that fit a user's calendar) on EBSN data, rather than isolated events. One of the only 2025+ papers applying LLMs to the event domain. Relevance: the closest academic sibling to our actual job ("find me something Friday evening" = constrained scheduling, not top-N ranking) — a framing classic event-rec papers ignore.
- **Language-Model Prior Overcomes Cold-Start Items — Wang, Ding, Kveton et al. (Amazon)** (paper, arXiv 2411.09065; https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09065) — LM embeddings of item text as Bayesian priors inside standard recommenders so brand-new zero-interaction items are rankable immediately; generic plug-in boosting sequential and CF recommenders on real datasets. Industry-authored, part of the now-standard LM-prior-for-cold-start recipe. Relevance: this IS the modern answer to the one-shot-event problem — embed event text/organizer/venue, transfer preference via embedding similarity. Cheap to implement, compatible with our OpenSearch function_score legacy design.
- **Large Language Models are Zero-Shot Rankers for Recommender Systems — Hou et al.** (paper, ECIR 2024, arXiv 2305.08845; https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08845) — LLMs prompted with user history + candidate list are competitive with (sometimes better than) trained recommenders as RE-RANKERS — but position-biased, popularity-biased, and poor at perceiving interaction order without prompt engineering. One of the most-cited LLM-recsys papers; spawned the retrieve-then-LLM-rerank standard. Relevance: blueprint for our stack — deterministic retrieval/filtering (geo, time window, source) then LLM re-rank + NL constraint matching; shuffle candidates and debias, or the LLM favors whatever is first/most famous.
- **LLMs as zero-shot point-of-interest recommenders** (paper, 2025; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395330451_Large_language_models_are_zero-shot_point-of-interest_recommenders) — Extends zero-shot LLM ranking to geo/POI recommendation — the nearest-neighbor domain to events (geo + venue + category, sparse per-item history). Single recent paper, niche. Relevance: evidence zero-shot LLM ranking transfers to geo-flavored one-shot items. Notably, no dedicated "LLM for event recommendation" literature exists as of mid-2026 — we get to define the recipe, and there is no academic accuracy bar competitors can cite against us.

### Web-agent benchmarks: capability curve

- **WebArena (CMU, 2023)** (active benchmark; https://benchmarkingagents.com/webarena/) — 812 tasks on self-hosted replica sites (shopping, forums, gitlab, maps). GPT-4 baseline 14.4% (2023); by early 2026 SOTA ~71-74% (ColorBrowserAgent 71.2%, tracked entries ~74.3%) vs ~78% human. Most-cited web-agent benchmark; 5-7x SOTA improvement 2023→2026. Relevance: the capability curve is real and steep — but WebArena is deterministic and login-free (no CAPTCHA, no bot-wall, no flaky network), so ~74% is an UPPER-bound proxy, not our operating number.
- **Online-Mind2Web / "An Illusion of Progress?" (OSU)** (active benchmark, paper Apr 2025, leaderboard live 2026; https://github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/Online-Mind2Web) — 300 realistic tasks on 136 LIVE websites. Showed ~90% WebVoyager scores collapse on the live web: 2025 — OpenAI Operator 61.3%, Claude Computer Use 3.7 56.3%, most agents below an early-2024 SeeAct baseline. Failure taxonomy: filter&sort 57.7% of failures, navigation 19.6%, incomplete multi-step 6.2%; CAPTCHA/bot-protection = categorical blockers. Mid-2026 leaderboard shows 90-97% claims (Browser Use Cloud 97.0%, GPT-5.4 native computer use 93.0%, ABP+Claude Opus 4.6 90.5%) — but the leaderboard itself warns judge methodology varies and scores are not directly comparable. De-facto live-web benchmark; its WebJudge auto-evaluator (85% human agreement) is now standard. Relevance: source of our existing ~56-64% planning number, now a 2025 floor; its failure taxonomy says agents break mostly on FILTERING/SORTING inside sites — precisely the step our architecture removes by doing discovery/filtering via APIs and using the browser only for the final RSVP click.
- **WebVoyager (2024) + Agent-E failure analysis** (benchmark, increasingly saturated; https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13919) — 643 tasks on 15 live sites incl. Booking.com. Original agent 59.1%; by 2025 Surfer 2 reports 97.1% and Surfer-H 92.2% at $0.13/task. Agent-E per-site: only 27.3% success on Booking.com tasks; of its booking failures 68.2% were "oblivious" (agent believed it succeeded) vs 4.5% self-aware. Relevance: (1) transactional/booking tasks are the WORST slice even when overall scores look great; (2) the dominant booking failure mode is silent false success — our RSVP layer must verify outcomes independently, never trust the agent's self-report. This is an explicit missing requirement to add to the spec.
- **REAL Bench (AGI Inc)** (active benchmark, v2 live 2026, arXiv 2504.11543; https://www.realevals.xyz/) — 112 tasks on deterministic high-fidelity replicas of 11-12 real sites — including OpenTable (reservation booking), Airbnb, DoorDash, Google Calendar, Gmail. Hosted, reproducible, open leaderboard; fast-growing 2025-2026. Relevance: the closest public benchmark to OUR task shape (find → reserve → confirm → calendar); a pre-launch eval harness and regression suite for prompt/model swaps in our browser-RSVP worker.

### Web-agent reliability and failure analysis

- **tau2-bench (Sierra, 2025-2026)** (active benchmark; https://leaderboard.steel.dev/leaderboards/tau-bench/) — Conversational transactional agents (airline/retail) that must gather user details and call write-APIs under policy. Introduced pass^k (ALL k attempts succeed): a 90% pass@1 agent falls to ~57% pass^8. Standard reliability benchmark. Relevance: quantifies why "usually works" is not a product — for a weekly-acting concierge, ~90% per-action success still means a bad week every couple of months; supports verify+retry+handoff on every autonomous action rather than chasing raw success rate.
- **WAREX: Web Agent Reliability Evaluation** (paper, arXiv 2510.03285; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03285) — Injects realistic network/server faults into existing benchmarks: agent success collapses 42%→2% under network faults, 42%→30% under server faults. Cited in 2026 reliability discussions. Relevance: benchmarks are best-case — real-world flakiness (slow venue sites, rate limits) craters success far below leaderboards. Our best-effort browser tier needs fault detection and graceful downgrade to handoff, budgeted as the COMMON path for long-tail sites, not the exception.
- **GAIA (Meta/HF, 2023) + 2026 leaderboard fragmentation** (active benchmark; https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/gaia) — 466 general-assistant tasks with tool use. Mid-2026: the SAME tasks score ~45% (bare models) to ~74.6% (Princeton HAL, Claude Sonnet 4.5) to 92%+ (heavily-scaffolded systems) depending on harness. Relevance: harness > model for assistant tasks — scaffolding, tools, and retry policy move scores 2x more than model choice; supports our Temporal-orchestrated, tool-first architecture over betting on a single smarter model.
- **"The Long-Horizon Task Mirage?"** (paper, arXiv 2604.11978, 2026; https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11978) — Seven-category empirically-grounded failure taxonomy for long-horizon agentic tasks; early errors compound across the plan→act→observe loop. Part of the post-leaderboard evaluation wave (with AgentAtlas, Holistic Agent Leaderboard). Relevance: our end-to-end flow (discover→rank→RSVP→calendar→reconcile) is exactly a long-horizon chain — checkpoint each stage with deterministic state (our Temporal spine already does) so early discovery errors can't silently propagate into a wrong RSVP.

## Verified findings

1. **[adjusted] Low ranking-accuracy ceiling from structural item cold start.** Event recommendation is structurally an item-cold-start problem with a low empirical accuracy ceiling in the classic literature: Macedo & Marinho (2014, Meetup data across 3 US cities, RSVP sparsity >99.99%) found the best classic algorithms (KNN and hybrid logistic regression) achieve NDCG@20 of at most ~0.3, far below mature recsys domains, because candidate events are always future items with little/no attendance trace when recommendations are needed (RSVPs cluster in the last ~20% of an event's lifetime). Context fusion (Macedo et al., RecSys 2015: social+geo+temporal features in a listwise ranker) improved NDCG@10 by up to 79% over baselines but absolute ranking quality remained modest. Caveats: the ~0.3 figure is a single 2014 study, not a proven ceiling for post-2015 neural methods, and quality rises substantially (NDCG@20 ~0.4-0.5) for the minority of events that accumulate >20 RSVPs. Practical meaning: a state-of-the-art ranker reliably puts the right event in a top-10 shortlist but cannot reliably pick THE one event. (Precision figures P≈0.35-0.45 attributed to the 2014 PDF could not be verified — treat as optimistic.)

2. **[adjusted] Signal hierarchy: group/social dominates; geo AND temporal are the weak tier.** Per Macedo et al. (RecSys 2015) and CIKM 2016 group-influence work, the strongest ranking signal is the user's existing group/social affiliations, followed by event-text content (which also helps cold-start events); geo distance and time-of-week/temporal habit are the two weakest individual signals (though still better than a popularity baseline) — the paper states verbatim that "the least informative contexts are the geographical and temporal ones." Combining all signals via learning-to-rank beats any single one, improving median NDCG@10 by 64-79% over the strongest baseline. Strategic upshot intact: group membership is both the dominant attendance predictor and the surface where autonomous RSVP is permitted.

3. **[adjusted] Whitespace: no dedicated LLM-for-event-recommendation literature, but two transferable validated techniques.** No dedicated LLM-for-event-recommendation paper surfaced as of mid-2026 — the LLM-recsys wave has skipped single-event recommendation, touching events only via adjacent work (CoS chain-of-scheduling for EBSN event schedules, Nov 2025; zero-shot POI recommenders, 2025). Classical EBSN research slowed rather than went silent after 2022 (e.g., 2023 heterogeneous-network/time-aware and 2025 graph-attention leisure-event papers). Two adjacent validated LLM techniques transfer directly: LM-embedding Bayesian priors for cold-start items (arXiv 2411.09065, Amazon) and zero-shot LLM re-ranking over deterministically retrieved candidates (Hou et al., ECIR 2024 — competitive with trained models, but with position- and popularity-bias failure modes requiring shuffling/debiasing). Nobody yet owns the deterministic-retrieval → LM-embedding similarity → LLM re-rank recipe for events (that recipe is our synthesis, not any single paper's prescription), but CoS signals the whitespace is beginning to close.

4. **[adjusted] Our ~56-64% autonomous-web-action number is a 2025 floor, not a ceiling — but the transactional slice stays far below headlines.** The 2025 Online-Mind2Web frontier (human-evaluated) was Claude Computer Use 3.7 = 56.3% and Operator = 61.3% (arXiv 2504.01382). Early-2026 (Feb-Mar) entries on the steel.dev leaderboard claim 90-97% on the same 300 live-web tasks (Browser Use Cloud 97.0%, GPT-5.4 native computer use 93.0%, ABP+Claude Opus 4.6 90.5%) — with explicit judge-methodology caveats (human eval vs WebJudge vs custom judges diverge; Operator's 61.3% was never independently verifiable). The transactional/booking slice remains far worse: Agent-E hit only 27.3% on Booking.com-style dynamic-form tasks; WAREX shows injected network faults collapse WebVoyager success from 42% to 2%; tau2-bench pass^k decays exponentially (90% pass@1 → ~57% pass^8). Note: 2026 frontier models now score ~90%+ pass@1 on tau2 retail/telecom, so the sub-50% worst-case argument rests on reliability decay and fault/anti-bot conditions, not tau2 pass@1 itself. Planning number: 75-90% happy-path on simple live-web actions; keep the honest-split architecture.

5. **[confirmed] Dominant web-agent failure modes: on-site filtering/sorting errors and silent false success.** "An Illusion of Progress" taxonomy: Filter&Sort 57.7% of failures, Navigation 19.6%, Incomplete Steps 6.2%, CAPTCHA/bot-protection as categorical blockers. Agent-E: 68.2% of booking failures were "oblivious" (agent believed it succeeded) vs 4.5% self-aware. Consequences: (1) our design already removes the worst failure class by doing discovery/filtering via APIs+search index and using the browser only for the terminal RSVP action; (2) independent outcome verification (confirmation-email parse / page-state check / calendar cross-check) is MANDATORY — the agent's own success report is the least trustworthy signal in the pipeline.

6. **(medium confidence, non-load-bearing) Matching eval infrastructure exists.** REAL Bench hosts deterministic replicas of OpenTable, Airbnb, DoorDash, Gmail, and Google Calendar (112 tasks, public leaderboard, v2 live 2026) — near 1:1 with our RSVP+calendar-write worker and usable as a regression suite for model/prompt swaps without hitting live sites. GAIA's 45%→92% harness-dependent spread on identical tasks shows harness/scaffolding moves outcomes more than model choice, supporting our tool-first Temporal architecture.

7. **(medium confidence, non-load-bearing) Group event recommendation is a mature, distinct subfield.** GMAN (2022), SRGAM (2020), ACGER solve preference aggregation, per-member influence weighting, and group cold start via graph attention over EBSN social edges. No surveyed commercial competitor productizes this — a defensible v2 "plan with friends" differentiator with the algorithm side de-risked by prior art.

## Product implications

- **Ranking architecture:** adopt the literature-converged stack — deterministic retrieval + hard filtering (geo radius, time window, source) in code/search-index; LM-embedding similarity to past attended events as the cold-start prior; LLM re-rank against the natural-language brief last, with candidate shuffling/debiasing. Never ask the browser agent to filter/sort on-site (57.7% of live-web agent failures are exactly that).
- **Honest positioning:** the classic-literature accuracy ceiling (NDCG@20 ~0.3 early-lifetime) means the promise is "a great explainable SHORTLIST of 3-10, one tap to commit" — not "we pick the one perfect event." The one-tap handoff doubles as the precision filter, converting the honest-split constraint into the product's accuracy mechanism.
- **Signal weighting:** existing group memberships > event-text content > geo distance ≈ time-of-week (both weak-tier alone, useful in fusion). This validates the launch wedge: Meetup groups the user already belongs to are simultaneously the highest-confidence recommendations AND the only surface where autonomous RSVP is permitted — lead marketing and onboarding with that surface.
- **Update the autonomous-action planning number:** 56-64% was the 2025 frontier; best 2026 agents plausibly reach 75-90% on simple live-web actions (90-97% leaderboard claims carry judge caveats), but transactional flows under real-world faults still fall below 50% worst-case (Booking-slice 27%, network-fault collapse 42%→2%). Keep the honest split; treat handoff as the budgeted common path for long-tail sources; re-benchmark quarterly — the curve is steep.
- **MISSING REQUIREMENT — independent RSVP outcome verification:** the dominant booking failure mode is silent false success (~68% of booking failures). Every autonomous RSVP must be confirmed via an out-of-band signal (confirmation-email parse, re-fetched page state, or source-API read-back) before the calendar write; failed verification auto-downgrades to the one-tap handoff.
- **MISSING REQUIREMENT — pass^k reliability engineering:** a weekly-acting concierge with 90% per-action success still fails a user monthly. Design verify+retry+handoff into every action; track pass^k-style repeated-trust metrics, not just per-action success rate.
- **Eval harness:** adopt REAL Bench (OpenTable/Airbnb/Gmail/Google Calendar replicas) as the standing regression suite for the browser-RSVP worker before any model or prompt swap; supplement with a small in-house Luma/Meetup/Eventbrite task set scored by a WebJudge-style LLM judge.
- **Onboarding:** user-side cold start is solved with explicit context elicitation, not waiting for behavioral data — ship a 60-second taste interview (topics, radius, weeknight/weekend windows, group-membership import) and treat RSVP-kept vs RSVP-bailed vs handoff-declined as the implicit feedback hierarchy thereafter.
- **Roadmap differentiators with prior art de-risking:** (a) group/"plan with friends" recommendation (graph-attention preference aggregation, unproductized by competitors); (b) calendar-constrained SCHEDULE construction per CoS (2025) — matches "Friday evening after work" queries better than top-N ranking and is unclaimed product territory.
