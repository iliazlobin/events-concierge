# Demand Signals, User Jobs & Willingness to Pay for Event Discovery/Booking

> Whether anyone actually pays for an autonomous event concierge — and at what price, for which job, for which user — determines the entire product opinion; this dossier assembles the demand evidence, WTP anchors, and failure precedents.

## Landscape inventory

### Incumbent discovery/ticketing platforms (demand-evidence sources + RSVP targets)

- **Eventbrite (TRNDS 2025 + Social Study 2026 reports)** (active; https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/press/newsroom/eventbrites-inaugural-social-study-report-reveals-the-reset-to-real/) — Largest self-serve ticketing/discovery marketplace; publishes the best public demand research on consumer event discovery (TRNDS 2025; Social Study Jan-2026, n=4,051 US+UK 18-35). Free events free to list, fees on paid tickets; acquired by Bending Spoons with fee/pricing turbulence expected. 33% of attendees vote it most useful ticketing platform for recommendations; its own data: 47% discover via word-of-mouth, 64% of Gen Z via social media. Primary demand-evidence source AND our biggest RSVP handoff target; its research proves discovery is social/fragmented — nobody, including Eventbrite, owns the "what should I do Friday" query.
- **Meetup (under Bending Spoons)** (active, decaying; https://www.meetup.com/blog/how-to-get-people-to-show-up-to-your-events/) — Interest-group event platform; the canonical free-RSVP source our concierge auto-RSVPs into. Organizer subscriptions; costs reportedly ~tripled since June 2024; floated $2-per-RSVP attendee fees. Documented organizer exodus (Trustpilot 2.3 stars, 694 reviews); organizer-reported no-show rates ~40% on free events. Two lessons: its decay + price hikes create the discovery vacuum we fill; and its 40-50% free-RSVP no-show pathology is the exact failure mode our auto-RSVP could worsen — organizers already ban serial no-shows.
- **Luma (lu.ma)** (active; https://luma.com/discover) — Event hosting + discovery beloved by the tech/AI scene; launched Luma Discovery (late 2024) and an AI Suite (June 2025). Free for hosts on basic, take rate on paid tickets (Luma Plus). ~2M people/month sign up for events; >$500M ticket sales and 250k active hosts by early 2025; claims its notification algorithms lift attendance to ~62% vs a ~40% industry average (marketing claim, unverified on a primary page). Our best-effort browser-RSVP target; the 62%-vs-40% claim suggests reminder/reconciliation loops measurably fix no-shows — a feature we must own since we, not the platform, hold the user relationship.
- **Fever (+ Secret Media Network, Atom Tickets, DICE)** (active, consolidating; https://newsroom.feverup.com/en-US/250537-fever-and-dice-join-forces-to-build-a-live-entertainment-tech-powerhouse/) — Curated-experiences giant: "Secret NYC"-style media funnels 60M+ monthly readers into Fever Originals (Candlelight etc.) at ~50% of box office; acquired Atom Tickets (Mar 2025) and DICE. $724M revenue (2025), ~$1.8-2B valuation, 125M community; wins the "things to do in X" SERP via Secret Media. Shows where the money is: owning curation + supply, not neutral aggregation. Our SERP competitor; we differentiate on cross-source neutrality + autonomous action, which Fever will never do (it steers to its own inventory).
- **DICE** (acquired by Fever, announced 2025; https://ra.co/news/82812) — Fan-friendly music ticketing with taste-based concert discovery, waiting lists, no hidden fees; UK/US/EU + India; live in 8 countries at acquisition; "turning point" year per IQ Magazine June 2026. Music-vertical proof that taste-ranked discovery + frictionless claim retains users; its waiting-list auto-buy mechanic is the closest existing thing to our auto-RSVP.

### Delegated-social / curated-matching products (WTP proof for "decide for me")

- **Timeleft** (active; https://timeleft.com/) — Algorithmically matches 5-6 strangers for weekly dinners; user does nothing but show up — the purest "delegate my social life" product. Subscription ~$20-26/mo in the US. €18M ARR (company-reported, ~20 months post-pivot), 3M+ users, 250+ cities / 52 countries, ~150k diners/month, ~6,500 dinners/week. Strongest WTP proof in the space: millions pay monthly to have an algorithm decide their social plans and commit them to a slot. Validates "decide for me + book me in" as the paid job, not search.
- **222** (active; https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/14/2440702/) — AI-curated group experiences with vetted strangers in 17 cities; personality-quiz matching to dinners/outings. $17 per-event curation fee, or $22/mo membership that waives fees. Pre-seed $1.45M (General Catalyst, YC); daily experiences. Prices the curation fee explicitly: people pay $17/event just for someone to pick the event and the people — our concierge does the same job across ALL event types; anchors a $15-25/mo tier.
- **Bumble For Friends (BFF)** (active; https://bumble.com/en/the-buzz/bumble-for-friends-bff-data-friendship-loneliness-online) — Friend-finding app spun from Bumble; 2025-26 redesign pivoted toward GROUP EVENTS as the friendship mechanism. Freemium + subscription. Category context: ~$16M US revenue, 4.3M downloads across top platforms (+28% YoY); remote workers = 34% of new friendship-app users. Segment evidence: remote workers and new-in-town adults are the paying core of the adjacent category, and even a dating giant concluded events (not chat) are how adults actually make friends — our concierge is the supply side of that job.

### Invite/RSVP and calendar layers (the Facebook Events vacuum)

- **Partiful** (active; https://www.cnbc.com/2025/04/19/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple.html) — Gen Z invite/RSVP layer for private parties; replaced Facebook Events for under-30s. Free (monetization nascent). +2M users in 2025; 500k MAU Q1-2025 (+400% YoY); 90% of US users aged 20-30; Google Best App 2024; TIME100 2025; provoked Apple Invites (Feb 2025). Proof the Facebook Events death left a real vacuum and RSVP UX is a wedge; also a discovery blind spot — private-graph events are invisible to any crawler, so our coverage story must be honest about public events only.
- **Howbout** (active; https://howbout.app/blog/apps-you-need/alternative-to-facebook-events/) — Shared social calendar app marketing itself explicitly as the answer to "Facebook Events don't work anymore". Free/freemium; niche but growing among UK/US Gen Z friend groups. Confirms the complaint corpus verbatim ("reach is dead, nobody RSVPs, every event spawns a WhatsApp group") and that the calendar is the sticky end-surface — matching our write-to-calendar terminal step.

### Curated local media / digest layer (curation-only price ceiling)

- **6AM City** (active; https://www.therebooting.com/p/6ams-playbook-for-sustainable-local) — Network of 25 local daily newsletters focused on "lighter fare" — events and things to do — skipping politics/crime. Free to readers, ad-supported; targets 50% margins per market in 2-3 years. ~1M cumulative subscribers, ~50% open rates, <$250k to launch a market. 50% open rates on things-to-do content is a screaming demand signal for a weekly digest surface — but ad-monetization shows readers don't pay for curation alone; supports a freemium digest + paid concierge-action split.
- **The Nudge** (active; https://www.nudgetext.com/) — SMS-first curated plans ("3 Nudges/week": what to do, where, cost, booking link) in SF, LA, Austin, NYC. Free core; Nudge Plus ~$5.79/mo billed annually ($9/mo monthly) for exclusive/seasonal plans, past-itinerary vault, local deals. 1M+ subscribers across 4 cities (Oct-2025 profile). Closest existing product to our digest layer, and it caps out at single-digit dollars — curation-only pricing is thin; the booking/RSVP execution we add justifies a higher tier. Also validates push (SMS) over pull (app browsing).

### Autonomous acquisition / delegation agents (the "agent acts for me" pricing ladder)

- **Appointment Trader** (active; https://appointmenttrader.com/) — Marketplace + AI concierge for buying/selling scarce restaurant reservations (also hotels, gov appointments); bots + humans snipe free-to-book slots and resell. 20-30% take on trades; tables sell $75 to $1,000+. ~$7M GMV in 2024 (up from $5.7M/yr as of May 2024); ~$20k/day purchase-volume peaks; NY passed a law against reservation resale (Feb 2025), which it is testing with an AI-chatbot relaunch. Hard proof people pay real money for AUTONOMOUS ACQUISITION of free-to-book scarce slots — the exact mechanic of our auto-RSVP for waitlisted/scarce events. Also flags the legal/organizer-hostility ceiling when bots consume free inventory.
- **Campnab** (active; https://campnab.com/pricing) — Monitors sold-out campgrounds and pings/books when cancellations appear — a booking sniper for a scarce free-ish resource. $10/$20/$30 per month subscription tiers plus $10-20 single-use scans; annual saves. Long-running profitable indie; a whole competitor category exists (Schnerp etc.). Cleanest WTP anchor for "agent watches + acts for me": $10-30/mo with a per-use option — directly transferable pricing model for scarce-event watch + auto-RSVP.
- **Duckbill** (active; https://getduckbill.com/) — AI + US-based human copilots execute personal to-do-list tasks end-to-end (bookings, research, errands). $49 Essentials / $99 Individual / $169 Household / $350 Household Plus per month. Forerunner-backed, founded 2022; positioned as "AI that actually gets sh*t done". Ceiling anchor for delegation WTP: affluent users pay $49-99+/mo when execution is guaranteed with a human backstop. Our browser-automation-with-human-handoff is the cheap-to-serve version of the same promise; supports a premium tier if reliability is proven.

### Cautionary corpses

- **Jukely** (dead — site no longer loads, though the domain still resolves; https://dancingastronaut.com/2017/09/jukely-alters-subscription-structure-caps-number-monthly-shows-subscribers/) — "Netflix of concerts": subscription to claim guest-list spots at shows; NYC launch 2014, ~15 cities. Historical pricing $25/mo (4 shows) → $35/5, $45/8, $65/10 after 2017 caps, Unlimited Plus $45/mo (unverified from live sources). Raised ~$24M; repeatedly cut benefits, signalling broken unit economics. Cautionary WTP lesson: people WILL pay ~$25/mo for event access, but subsidizing inventory (unsold tickets) breaks when supply is inconsistent. We sell labor/decisions, not inventory — structurally avoiding Jukely's margin trap.
- **IRL** (dead, 2023; https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/26/irl-shut-down-fake-users/) — Event-discovery social app for Gen Z ("Facebook Events replacement"); pivoted to online events during COVID; unicorn at $1.17B. Free. Raised >$200M (SoftBank-led $170M Series C); shut down after the board found 95% of claimed 20M MAU were bots; SEC investigation. Two lessons: VCs believed the event-discovery thesis enough to fund a unicorn — the demand narrative is fundable; and engagement in pure discovery apps is so weak the founder faked it. Retention must come from the booked outcome + calendar, not browsing.

### Trend/framing research

- **Eventbrite "Fourth Spaces" report** (paper, Jan 2025; https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250128081264/en/Eventbrites-New-Report-Reveals-Emergence-of-Fourth-Spaces-As-Gen-Z-and-Millennials-Look-to-Bridge-Their-Digital-and-Physical-Worlds) — Trend report coining "fourth spaces": events as the bridge between digital and physical community for Gen Z/millennials. Framing ammunition for positioning: events are being re-cast as social infrastructure, not entertainment — the concierge sells belonging logistics, not tickets.

## Verified findings

1. **Discovery is fragmented and social-first; no platform owns the "what should I do" query.** [adjusted] Word-of-mouth leads (47% of consumers discover events via word of mouth, Eventbrite TRNDS 2025; 69% of 18-35s rely on personal networks, Eventbrite Social Study 2026, n=4,051 US+UK, fielded Jul 2025); 64% of Gen Z / 61% of millennials use social media to find things to do (TRNDS 2025); ~30% of Gen Z use TikTok specifically for event discovery (TRNDS 2025); and only 26% called ticketing platforms the most useful recommendation source — note that last figure is from Eventbrite's Summer 2023 trends survey, ~3 years stale, though directionally consistent. Pew (Nov 2025) corroborates the channel shift: roughly half of 18-29s use TikTok daily. The "Facebook Events is dead" complaint corpus (Howbout, r/city threads) plus Meetup's post-acquisition organizer exodus confirm the old aggregators lost the job. This is precisely the fragmentation an agent that watches ALL sources monetizes.

2. **Strong, current demand tailwind: loneliness epidemic + third-place decline is pushing 18-35s to plan MORE in-person events.** [confirmed] 79% of 18-35s (US and UK, not US-only) plan to attend more events in 2026 and 89% want events that connect them to community (Eventbrite Social Study 2026); ~25% of 18-29s report loneliness (Meta-Gallup; US-cohort surveys run higher, so this if anything understates the tailwind) and ~half of US adults experience loneliness per the Surgeon General's 2023 advisory (heart disease +29%, stroke +32% risk framing); rapid third-place decline 2019-2021 per a census-tract study still front-page in July 2026 (Axios). Category momentum is monetizing now: Timeleft €18M ARR, friendship-app downloads +28% YoY.

3. **Willingness to pay attaches to OUTCOMES (a booked slot, a decided plan), not discovery/aggregation.** [adjusted] The verified pricing ladder: ~$5.79/mo annual ($9/mo monthly) for curation-only (The Nudge Plus); ~$20/mo for curated matching (222: $17 per-event fee or $22/mo membership that waives it); ~$20-26/mo for decide-and-book dinners (Timeleft, company-reported €18M ARR after 20 months); $10-30/mo subscriptions or $10-20 per-use for autonomous watch-and-book agents (Campnab); roughly $75 to $1,000+ per scarce free-to-book slot (Appointment Trader, ~$7M GMV in 2024 — note NY banned reservation resales in spring 2025 and other states are following); and $49-$350/mo for human-backstopped full task delegation (Duckbill; $99/mo Individual tier). Pure discovery products died (IRL: >$200M raised, 95% of reported users were bots) or stayed free/ad-supported (6AM City, city newsletters). Jukely — which subsidized ticket inventory at historical $25-65/mo tiers (unverified) — is dead: its site no longer loads, though the domain still resolves. The ladder is remarkably consistent: information ≤$5-10/mo; curation + commitment ~$20/mo; autonomous acquisition $10-30/mo or per-unit fees; guaranteed execution $49-99+/mo. Our concierge sits in the $15-30/mo band: it sells decisions + booked outcomes without buying inventory.

4. **Free-RSVP no-shows run roughly 40-60%; money fixes it, reminders only partially; an auto-RSVP agent without an attendance loop amplifies the ecosystem's worst pathology.** [adjusted] Meetup's official blog states free-event show-up rates of 40-50%, lifting to 70-85% when a fee is attached (these percentages live on Meetup's blog, not the oft-cited Medium post, which does however confirm host no-show-ban policies); organizers quoted in Eventbrite's own guidance report 25-50% dropout at free events; even a $3-5 refundable deposit cuts no-shows to ~15%. Reminders alone help less: Luma's ~62%-vs-40% attendance claim is marketing-grade and unverified on a primary page. Serial no-shows get barred from Meetup groups, so unclosed auto-RSVPs risk getting our users banned. Regulatory context: New York's Restaurant Reservation Anti-Piracy Law (Feb 2025) bans unauthorized third-party RESALE of reservations — not free-RSVP bots per se — with Appointment Trader testing it via an AI-chatbot relaunch; a clear signal that agent-driven over-claiming of scarce slots draws regulation. Implication: our spec needs a first-class attendance-confirmation + auto-un-RSVP mechanism; the current discover→rank→RSVP→calendar pipeline treats reconciliation as organizer-side only, not user-side intent decay.

5. **The wedge user is the 25-40 urban "socially displaced" adult.** [unverifiable — medium researcher confidence] New-in-town movers, remote workers (34% of new friendship-app users), and post-friend-group millennials: they have money, intent (79% plan more events), and no discovery graph. Convergent evidence: Timeleft's paying base skews 28-45 urban professionals; Meetup's classic base is the same demographic; Duckbill's $99 delegation sells to busy professionals; Eventbrite finds $30 the ticket-price sweet spot with 55% calling affordability very important — they'll spend on the event plus a modest service layer. Critically, Social Study 2026 shows 79% value spontaneity BUT 51% still need detailed logistics handled — the exact "concierge does 95%, you tap once" promise. Gen Z, by contrast, discovers via TikTok, enjoys the hunt (36%), and expects free — a worse first customer.

6. **The "things to do in X" search/SEO channel is already owned by media brands monetizing attention, not outcomes.** [medium confidence, not load-bearing] Fever's Secret Media Network (60M+ monthly uniques) funnels the SERP into Fever's ~50%-margin Originals ($724M 2025 revenue — evidence the intent is enormous but competing head-on is capital-intensive); 6AM City gets ~50% open rates on 1M newsletter subscribers; Time Out and city outlets fill the rest. The open lane is the layer none of them will build because it conflicts with their inventory economics: neutral cross-source ranking plus autonomous RSVP on OTHER platforms' events.

7. **Decision-fatigue framing beats FOMO framing among 18-35s.** [high confidence, not load-bearing] 52% prefer under-the-radar events, 49% want things "less curated and more real", 79% value spontaneity over perfect planning, yet 51% need logistics fully handled (Eventbrite Social Study 2026 — the "Reset to Real" cohort resents both algorithmic over-curation and planning overhead). A concierge should present few, confident picks (1-3 per ask) with logistics pre-solved rather than a comprehensive FOMO feed — matching The Nudge's success shipping exactly 3 plans/week to 1M subscribers rather than a browseable catalog.

8. **VC and consolidation activity confirms a hot 2025-26 category concentrating on supply and invites, leaving the demand-side agent lane empty.** [medium confidence, not load-bearing] Fever raised $100M and bought Atom Tickets + DICE; Bending Spoons bought Meetup AND Eventbrite; Partiful provoked Apple Invites; Luma shipped an AI discovery suite. Every major mover monetizes the organizer/inventory side (take rates, subscriptions, ads); a user-side agent that RSVPs across all of them is structurally misaligned with each incumbent's captive-discovery model — the classic neutral-third-party wedge, with platform ToS hostility (already priced into our technical corpus) as the incumbents' main defense.

9. **Jukely — the only prior "subscription that gets you into events" at scale — is defunct.** [adjusted — medium confidence] Its site no longer loads (connection hangs), though the domain DOES still resolve via CloudFront, contra the original "DNS unresolvable" observation; its 2017 restructuring (capping "unlimited" at 4-10 shows) had already signaled inventory-subsidy economics failing, and its historical $25-65/mo pricing could not be independently verified from live sources. Distinguish carefully in the product opinion: Jukely sold discounted ACCESS (it ate ticket-cost risk); we sell LABOR (discovery + booking effort) — different cost structure, same customer instinct.

## Product implications

- **POSITIONING: sell the outcome, not the search** — "you have plans Friday; we did the finding, deciding, and signing up." Decision-fatigue framing ("remove the planning tax", 51% want logistics handled) beats FOMO framing ("never miss out"); the Eventbrite 2026 cohort explicitly resents over-curated feeds. Present 1-3 confident picks per ask, never a browseable catalog (The Nudge's 3-plans/week at 1M subscribers is the pattern; IRL's browse-feed death is the anti-pattern).
- **WEDGE USER: 25-40 urban "socially displaced"** — new-in-town movers, remote workers (34% of friendship-app signups), post-friend-group millennials. They have intent (79% plan more events in 2026), money ($30 ticket sweet spot + service-layer budget), and no local discovery graph. Do NOT chase Gen Z first: they discover via TikTok, enjoy the hunt (36%), and expect free.
- **PRICING: anchor a paid tier at $15-25/mo** — inside the proven band (222 $22/mo membership, Timeleft ~$20-26/mo dinners, Campnab $10-30/mo watch-and-book) — with a free weekly digest tier (50% open-rate appetite proven by 6AM City; curation alone caps at ~$5-9/mo, never sell it standalone). Consider a per-success fee ($5-15) for scarce/waitlisted event acquisition, Appointment-Trader-style, and keep a future $79-99/mo human-backstopped "guaranteed booking" tier in the roadmap (Duckbill proves the ceiling; note its full delegation actually starts at $49/mo). Never subsidize ticket inventory (Jukely's fatal flaw).
- **MISSING REQUIREMENT — ATTENDANCE LOOP (highest-priority spec gap):** free-RSVP no-shows run 40-60% and auto-RSVP would make us the worst offender at scale. Add: T-24h attendance-confirmation nudge; decline/no-response → automatic un-RSVP; per-user cap on concurrent open RSVPs; per-source pacing; a user "attendance score" that throttles auto-RSVP aggressiveness. This is also organizer-trust PR armor — Meetup organizers already ban serial no-shows, and NY legislated against reservation resale after agent over-claiming (Appointment Trader precedent).
- **MISSING REQUIREMENT — SOCIAL-SIGNAL INGESTION:** for the launch segment, platform listings suffice, but the roadmap must acknowledge TikTok/Instagram as the de-facto Gen Z discovery channel (~30% of Gen Z find events on TikTok) and private-graph events (Partiful, +2M users in 2025) as a permanent coverage blind spot. Be honest in marketing that we cover PUBLIC events, and consider user-forwarded links (paste a TikTok/IG/Partiful link → concierge extracts + calendars it) as a cheap v1 bridge.
- **MISSING REQUIREMENT — SOCIAL PROOF / +1 MECHANICS:** word-of-mouth dominates discovery (47-69%), and Bumble BFF pivoted to group events because events are how adults make friends. Even a single-user v1 should support "invite a friend to this pick" (forwardable event card with its own one-tap RSVP handoff) — it doubles as the growth loop in a category where every incumbent grew via invites (Partiful, Luma).
- **GO-TO-MARKET:** don't fight Fever/Time Out/6AM City for the "things to do in X" SERP (Fever's 60M-uniques Secret Media owns it and steers to owned inventory). Instead ride the loneliness/third-places narrative (still front-page July 2026, Axios) with the neutral-agent story no inventory-owner can copy, and distribute through channels the wedge user already trusts: city subreddits, local newsletters (6AM City ad slots), and new-in-town moments (mover partnerships).
- **TRUST RULE:** never inflate activity or RSVP on the user's behalf beyond confirmed intent — IRL burned $200M and the category's credibility on fake engagement. Verifiably high-intent RSVPs (via the attendance loop above) convert the no-show liability into our differentiating quality signal for organizers ("concierge users show up").
